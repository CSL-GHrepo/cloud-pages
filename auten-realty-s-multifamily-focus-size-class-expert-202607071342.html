Capital Markets and Financing Insights for Multifamily Investors

Traditional Financing Options

Fixed-rate and variable-rate loans from banks, credit unions, and government-backed entities like Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, or the FHA remain popular for multifamily acquisitions. Key considerations include:

  • Loan-to-Value (LTV): Typically ranging from 70-80% for Class A and B properties, impacting upfront equity requirements.
  • Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR): Lenders usually require a DSCR above 1.20 to ensure cash flow adequacy for loan payments.
  • Loan Terms: Amortization periods often span 25-30 years with 5-10 year fixed-rate terms, offering predictability and stability.

Private Equity and Bridge Financing

For acquisitions requiring swift capital deployment or non-traditional underwriting, private equity funds and bridge lenders supplement traditional financing avenues. These sources offer flexibility but may come at higher cost, suited for value-add or repositioning plays with defined exit strategies.

  • Equity Partnerships: Syndicating capital to pool resources and share risk among investors.
  • Bridge Loans: Short-term financing to acquire or refinance assets prior to stabilization or permanent mortgage placement.
  • Mezzanine Debt: Subordinate loans that fill capital gaps and enable higher leverage.

Auten Realty coordinates introductions to trusted capital partners familiar with the unique college town multifamily market dynamics, streamlining deal execution.
