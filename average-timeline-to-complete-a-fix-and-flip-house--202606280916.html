Quick Answer: How Long Does a Fix and Flip Take?

On average, completing a fix and flip property takes between 3 to 6 months. This timeline includes acquiring the property, planning, permitting, renovation, inspections, and finally resale. Industry data indicates that the renovation phase alone generally accounts for 60-70% of the total project timeline.

“Industry professionals recommend allocating a minimum of 90 days for the rehab phase, with an overall 4 to 6 month timeline to allow for unforeseen delays and holding costs.” – National Real Estate Investors Association

Understanding the Fix and Flip Process Steps

The fix and flip timeline is driven by a well-defined sequence of project phases. Below is a typical breakdown of the fix and flip project phases, highlighting average durations:

  1. Property Acquisition and Inspection (1-2 weeks): Finding the right property is often the most uncertain part but once identified, inspections and due diligence typically take 7-14 days.
  2. Planning and Permitting (2-4 weeks): Developing a detailed renovation plan including design, budgeting, and obtaining necessary permits from local authorities.
  3. Renovation and Rehab (8-12 weeks): The core fix and flip timeline: includes demolition, structural repairs, system upgrades (plumbing, electrical), cosmetic finishes, and landscaping.
  4. Final Inspection, Staging, and Listing (2-3 weeks): Completing final walk-throughs, staging the home for better market appeal, and getting it listed to attract buyers.
  5. Sale and Closing (4-6 weeks): Negotiation, contract finalization, and closing the sale.

These phases can overlap or extend based on project complexity, contractor reliability, and market conditions.

Breaking Down Property Rehab Duration: Real-World Example

Consider a mid-sized single-family home rehab in a typical suburban market. Based on firsthand experience managing fix and flip projects:

  • Week 1-2: Purchase complete, finalizing inspection report and project scope.
  • Week 3-4: Permits secured; contractors mobilize to start demolition and rough-ins.
  • Week 5-10: Major construction phase including HVAC upgrade, electrical rewiring, plumbing repairs, drywall installation and painting.
  • Week 11-12: Flooring, cabinetry installation, appliances, fixtures, and landscaping.
  • Week 13: Final inspections, walkthrough, and staging.
  • Week 14-16: Marketing, open houses, and eventual sale closing.

This streamlined schedule led to a total property rehab duration of about 4 months. Even with minor delays in material delivery, proactive project management helped keep the timeline on track.

Tips for Reducing Your Fix and Flip Timeline

Industry experts emphasize the following to minimize project duration and prevent timeline inflation:

  • Conduct thorough pre-purchase inspections: Identify hidden issues early to avoid surprise delays.
  • Secure all permits before construction begins: Permitting bottlenecks cause the most frequent hold-ups.
  • Hire experienced contractors familiar with renovation project timelines: Proven track records correlate with speed and quality.
  • Maintain a detailed house renovation schedule with daily milestones: Use project management tools to track progress.
  • Order materials in advance: Avoid supply chain delays commonly experienced during renovation phases.
  • Have contingency plans and budget buffers: Unexpected issues often arise; being prepared limits disruption.

Regional Considerations

While the above timelines provide a general framework, regional factors can influence property rehab duration. For example:

  • Urban areas often have longer permitting timelines compared to rural locations.
  • Climate and seasonality affect exterior work schedules like roofing or landscaping.
  • Material availability and labor market conditions can vary broadly across regions.

Understanding your local market nuances is essential to customizing your fix_and_flip timeline.
