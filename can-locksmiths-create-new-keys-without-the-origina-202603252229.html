Understanding Lock and Security Device Terminology

Some common terms you'll hear include 'deadbolt,' which provides added security against forced entry by extending a bolt into the door frame. Deadbolts are a staple in residential security. Another is 'rekeying,' the process of changing the internal pins of a lock so that a new key operates it—useful during property ownership changes. 'Electronic locks' refer to keyless systems that use codes, cards, or biometrics instead of traditional keys, offering convenience and enhanced control. Knowing these terms helps in making informed decisions when upgrading your security system in Parkland.

‘High-security locks’ are specialized hardware designed to resist picking, drilling, and other forced entry methods. They often feature complex keyways and reinforced materials. ‘Access control systems’ are electronic systems that regulate who can enter certain areas, often with card readers or biometric scans. Finally, ‘rekeying’ is an affordable way to change your keys’ access without replacing entire locksets. Familiarity with these terms ensures you understand the options available and can communicate effectively with your locksmith.

For example, upgrading to a high-security lock like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock can significantly improve your protection, especially if you’re concerned about break-ins. Electronic locks with keypad entry are popular in commercial settings or high-end homes, providing keyless convenience. When consulting with a locksmith in Parkland, always ask about these terms and what solutions best fit your situation. Using the right terminology helps ensure you get the exact service you need and avoid costly misunderstandings.

Additionally, understanding standard certifications such as the Grade 1 or Grade 2 rating from the American National Standards Institute (ANSI) helps assess the quality of locks. Grade 1 locks are the most secure and durable, often used in commercial applications, while Grade 2 is suitable for residential use.
