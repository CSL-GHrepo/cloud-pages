Tax Implications for Sellers in Cash Home Sales in Parkland, FL

Selling your home for cash in Parkland comes with important tax considerations that sellers must not overlook. Proper understanding of capital gains, exemptions, and reporting requirements can help avoid unexpected liabilities.

Capital Gains Tax and Home Sale Exemptions

If you sell your primary residence for a profit, the IRS allows exclusions under certain conditions. Florida has no state income tax, but federal rules still apply:

  • Exemption Limits: Up to $250,000 in capital gains exclusion for single filers, or $500,000 for married couples filing jointly, provided you’ve owned and lived in the home for at least 2 of the past 5 years.
  • Reporting Requirements: Sellers must report sales on IRS Form 8949 and Schedule D if the sale doesn’t qualify for exclusion or if the gain exceeds the exemption.
  • Taxable Gains: Any profit beyond the exemption may be subject to federal capital gains tax rates, depending on income and filing status.

1031 Exchanges: An Alternative for Investment Properties

Sellers of Parkland rental or investment properties may defer capital gains taxes through a 1031 exchange, which allows reinvestment of proceeds into similar properties within specified timeframes. However, this is typically not applicable to primary residences.
