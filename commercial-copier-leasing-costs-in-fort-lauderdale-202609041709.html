Evaluating Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Beyond Lease Payments

While the monthly lease payment is often the primary focus for businesses, assessing the total cost of ownership (TCO) of a commercial copier provides a clearer picture of its financial impact over the lease term. TCO includes not only the lease payments themselves but also ancillary costs associated with operation, maintenance, and eventual replacement.

Components of Total Cost of Ownership

  • Lease Payments: The fixed or variable monthly fee agreed upon in the lease contract.
  • Consumables: Toner, ink cartridges, drum units, staples, and paper — some may be included but others might be billed separately depending on the lease.
  • Maintenance & Repairs: Proactive service visits and unplanned repairs. Contracts can differ widely in coverage.
  • Utilities: Electrical consumption varies by machine size and usage; high-speed color copiers generally consume more power.
  • End-of-Lease Charges: Potential fees for excessive wear, damage, or exceeding print volumes.
  • Training & User Support: Initial and ongoing training for staff to optimize efficient machine use may have costs or require time.
  • Downtime Impact Costs: Productivity loss during repairs or maintenance can indirectly increase business expenses.

Businesses that incorporate these elements into their budgeting process will gain a better understanding of the real impact of leasing decisions on operational costs.

Example: Calculating TCO for a Mid-Range Copier

Suppose a medium-sized insurance firm leases a black-and-white multifunction copier at $200/month over 36 months. Included are maintenance and toner, but excess prints incur $0.02 per page after the 10,000 monthly page limit.

  • Base lease costs: $200 × 36 = $7,200
  • Excess prints: averaging 1,000 pages monthly over limit → 1,000 × $0.02 × 36 = $720
  • Electricity estimate: $15/month × 36 = $540
  • Training costs: one-time $300
  • Estimated downtime loss: minimal due to responsive on-site support

Total estimated TCO: $7,200 + $720 + $540 + $300 = $8,760 over three years.

Comparing TCO across different lease options and vendors with a full breakdown helps avoid surprising expenses later.
