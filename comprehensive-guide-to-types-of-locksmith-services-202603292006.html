Understanding Common Lock and Security Terminology

To make informed decisions about your security, it helps to understand some basic locksmith terms. One common term is 'rekeying', which involves changing the internal pins of a lock so a new key operates it, without replacing the entire lock. This is often a cost-effective security upgrade after moving into a new home or losing keys. Another term you might hear is 'deadbolt', a sturdy lock mechanism that provides additional security against forced entry. Knowing the difference between single-cylinder and double-cylinder deadbolts can improve your home protection measures. — Lockguard Locksmiths serves the greater Parkland area.

A 'Master Key System' allows multiple locks to be operated by a single key, providing convenience and control in commercial properties. 'High-security locks' are designed with complex keyways and resistant materials, making them harder to pick or drill. 'Access control systems' enable remote or electronic management of who can enter specific areas—useful for businesses in Parkland's commercial zones. Understanding these terms helps you communicate more effectively with your locksmith and choose the right security solutions. It's also worth exploring how technology like smart locks can integrate with your existing security system.

For homeowners, 'key duplication' and 'lock installation' are common services that enhance security. Knowing the differences between different lock types, such as mortise locks or keypad locks, can guide your upgrade choices. The term 'bump key' refers to a technique used to open pin-tumbler locks; a reputable locksmith will advise on anti-bump features to prevent this method.
