Quick Answer: What is Copier Leasing and How Does It Work?

Copier leasing is a contract-based service where a business rents copying machines for a set duration, typically ranging from one to five years. Instead of purchasing outright, leasing provides access to top-of-the-line office copiers with included maintenance and upgrades under flexible copier agreements. This allows businesses to preserve capital, benefit from cost-effective copier solutions, and adapt quickly to changes in technology or print volume.

“Leasing office equipment, including copiers, can improve cash flow by allocating expenses evenly and avoiding the large upfront costs of buying.” – Industry Expert, Business Equipment Leasing Association

What is Copier Leasing? A Detailed Explanation

At its core, commercial copier leasing is a form of business equipment leasing where a company uses a copier without actually owning it. Instead of an upfront purchase, the business enters a lease agreement with a copier vendor or leasing company. These agreements typically last between 12 and 60 months and include monthly payments based on the copier's value and the contract terms.

Leasing agreements usually cover:

  • The right to use the copier hardware
  • Ongoing maintenance and repairs (copier maintenance included)
  • Access to technical support and software updates
  • Options to upgrade equipment mid-lease or at the end of the term

This model contrasts with buying, where the business must pay the full purchase price upfront, and maintenance costs are the owner's responsibility.

The Process of Copier Leasing: Step-by-Step

Understanding how copier leasing works in practice can help you make an informed decision. Here’s a typical process:

  1. Assessment: Evaluate your business needs, including monthly print volume, required features (color printing, scanning, faxing), and office space considerations.
  2. Consultation: Engage with a leasing company to discuss options and receive tailored leasing plan proposals.
  3. Proposal and Agreement: Review lease vs buy copier costs, select a flexible copier agreement, and sign the contract detailing payment terms, duration, and included services.
  4. Installation: The leasing company delivers and sets up the copier(s), often providing training for staff.
  5. Ongoing Use and Maintenance: Use the copier with full maintenance support included. The leasing company handles repairs, parts replacement, and updates during the lease term.
  6. Lease End Options: At the end of the term, choose to upgrade, renew, purchase, or return the copier equipment based on your business goals.

Flexible copier agreements often allow businesses to scale their copier fleet or switch to newer models easily, which is a significant advantage over outright ownership.

Popular Types of Copier Leasing Plans

A variety of affordable copier plans cater to different business sizes and usage patterns, such as:

  • Fair Market Value (FMV) Lease: Allows you to lease with flexible end-of-term options including upgrades or returns.
  • Fixed Lease: Fixed monthly payments for the entire term, often with the option to purchase at lease end.
  • Copy Volume-Based Lease: Payments based on your actual copy volume, ideal for fluctuating workloads.
  • All-Inclusive Lease: Covers hardware, supplies, maintenance, and sometimes even software solutions.

Your choice depends on budget preferences, anticipated print usage, and desired flexibility. Leasing experts recommend analyzing your print data and growth plans before selecting a plan.

Financial Considerations: Impact on Business Credit and Balance Sheets

Leasing copiers not only smooths cash flow but also affects how equipment expenses are reflected in a company’s financial statements and credit profile. Understanding these impacts can help CFOs and business owners make more informed choices.

Off-Balance-Sheet Financing

Traditionally, operating leases allowed businesses to keep leased equipment off their balance sheets. This means:

  • The leased copier does not appear as a fixed asset.
  • Lease payments are treated as operating expenses on the income statement.
  • Liabilities are typically lower, improving debt-to-equity and liquidity ratios.

This treatment enhances financial metrics important to lenders and investors by preserving borrowing capacity and demonstrating lean asset management.

Accounting Standards Update (ASC 842 and IFRS 16)

Recent accounting standard changes require most leases to be recognized as assets and liabilities on balance sheets. This still includes:

  • Recording a “right-of-use” asset representing your leased copier.
  • Recording an associated lease liability showing the present value of future lease payments.

Despite this, leasing remains advantageous because it avoids large capital outlays and can provide predictable expense recognition. Consultation with financial advisors and accountants is recommended to understand the precise impact for your company under current accounting rules.

Effect on Business Credit

Consistent, timely lease payments reflect positively on business credit scores, signaling fiscal responsibility. Leasing companies may also structure agreements to avoid negatively impacting borrowing limits or credit lines, unlike heavier fixed asset financing.

How to Evaluate Copier Leasing Providers: Key Questions to Ask

Choosing the right leasing partner is crucial. Beyond price, thorough provider evaluation ensures you get reliable service and optimal terms. Here are essential questions to pose during your selection process:

  • What maintenance services are included, and how quickly are service calls responded to? Ensure SLAs (Service Level Agreements) meet your business uptime requirements.
  • Are supplies like toner and staples included or charged separately? Clarify total cost of ownership to avoid surprises.
  • What are the upgrade options mid-lease and at lease end? Confirm flexibility aligned with your growth plans.
  • How transparent is the billing? Are there any hidden fees or overage charges? Demand clarity and detailed invoicing.
  • Does the provider handle equipment disposal and recycling at lease end? Confirm compliance with environmental standards.
  • Are there references or case studies available demonstrating service quality and customer satisfaction? Third-party testimonials are invaluable.

Taking the time to vet providers thoroughly will pay dividends in service quality and peace of mind during the lease term.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does copier leasing include besides the machine itself?

Copier leasing typically includes the hardware, full maintenance and repairs, toner and supplies, and technical support. This all-in-one service ensures your copier remains operational without hidden costs. Some leases also cover software updates and offer options for future equipment upgrades.

How do I decide between leasing or buying a copier for my office?

The choice depends on your financial situation and business needs. Leasing generally offers lower upfront costs, flexible agreements, and maintenance inclusion, making it ideal for growing or financially cautious businesses. Buying provides asset ownership and may be better for companies with consistent print volumes and capital availability.

Can copier leasing agreements be customized?

Yes, many leasing companies offer flexible copier agreements tailored to your copy volume, lease term, and service requirements. Plans can range from fixed monthly payments to volume-based billing, allowing you to optimize costs based on actual use.

Are there any tax benefits related to leasing copiers?

Often, lease payments can be fully deducted as a business expense, offering tax advantages by lowering taxable income. This contrasts with buying, where depreciation schedules apply. It’s recommended to consult with a tax professional for advice specific to your location and situation.
