Understanding the Cost Difference: Copier Leasing vs Buying

At first glance, buying a copier seems more straightforward: pay once, own the machine forever. However, the upfront cost can be steep. High-end commercial copiers often cost between $10,000 to $30,000 depending on features and volume capacity. Added to this, ongoing maintenance, toner, and repairs cause long-term expenses to accumulate.

Conversely, copier leasing spreads out costs evenly over a fixed term, usually 3 to 5 years. Monthly payments often include maintenance and upgrades, lowering unexpected expenses. According to Forbes, businesses that lease office equipment like copiers can save an average of 20% in total cost of ownership due to reduced maintenance and upgrade flexibility.1

“Leasing commercial copiers offers a strategic advantage by reducing upfront capital outlay and smoothing cash flow, while providing access to the latest technology without large upgrade investments.” – Jane Mitchell, Office Equipment Industry Analyst

  • Buying upfront costs: $10,000 to $30,000+, including installation and initial supplies.
  • Leasing monthly costs: Often $200–$600, with service and supplies included.
  • Long-term copier expenses: Higher when buying due to maintenance, repair, and replacement risks.
  • Technology refresh: Easier with leasing, as terms allow equipment upgrades mid-contract.

Buying Vs Leasing Copiers: The Long-Term Expense Breakdown

When analyzing buying vs leasing copiers in terms of long-term copier expenses, it’s essential to factor in total cost of ownership (TCO): purchase price, depreciation, maintenance, supplies, downtime, and resale value.

  • Depreciation: Purchased copiers depreciate quickly, losing around 50% of value in 3 years as technology advances.
  • Maintenance and repairs: Buyers often face unpredictable costs; leased units typically include these in the agreement.
  • Downtime impact: Leased copiers usually offer faster service response, reducing lost productivity.
  • Upgrade options: Ownership locks a company into outdated models, while leasing enables easier transitions.

Industry data indicates that businesses that lease commercial copiers can reduce unexpected downtime costs by 30%, thanks to included service guarantees.2

Commercial Copier Options and Office Equipment Financing Explained

Choosing the right commercial copier is only half the battle. Financing options, including leasing and buying, play a critical role in managing office budgets.

Office equipment financing comes in forms such as:

  • Capital purchase: Pay full price upfront; ideal for companies with ample capital and plans for long-term use.
  • Operating lease: Similar to renting, with lower upfront costs and service included.
  • Lease-to-own: Combines leasing with eventual ownership after contract completion.

Businesses in dynamic markets often prefer leasing for flexibility and cost efficiency copier solutions. Large enterprises, on the other hand, may capitalize on bulk purchase discounts but must allocate budget for potential maintenance spikes and technology refreshment.
