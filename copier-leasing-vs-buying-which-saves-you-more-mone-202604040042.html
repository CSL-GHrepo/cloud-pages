Purchasing vs Leasing Copiers: A Cost Comparison

When weighing purchasing versus leasing, consider these financial factors:

  1. Upfront Capital Investment: Buying requires a significant initial expense, impacting cash flow, especially for small to medium businesses.
  2. Depreciation and Asset Value: Copiers depreciate rapidly. Ownership means absorbing the loss in value, while leasing shifts this risk to the leasing company.
  3. Maintenance and Repairs: Buyers bear full responsibility for servicing, which can be unpredictable and costly over time.
  4. Technological Obsolescence: Purchasing locks you into a device that may become outdated, while leasing enables easier transitions to newer models.

According to a 2023 survey by TechAdvisory Group, companies that purchase copiers spent on average 35% more in total long-term copier costs—including maintenance and upgrades—compared to those who opt for leasing agreements.

Long-Term Copier Costs: Beyond the Sticker Price

It's important to evaluate the total amount paid over the copier's lifecycle rather than focusing only on initial costs. Factors to consider include:

  • Consumable supplies: Toner, paper, and parts vary in cost depending on the model and age.
  • Service disruptions: Repairs can lead to downtime impacting employee productivity.
  • Software and firmware upgrades: Essential for security and performance but sometimes costly if purchased separately.

Leasing typically bundles these expenses into predictable payments, reducing the financial risk of unexpected copier maintenance expenses. Many businesses find lease agreements the most budget-friendly copier solutions over time.
