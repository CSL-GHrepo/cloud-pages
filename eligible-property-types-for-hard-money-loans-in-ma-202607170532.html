Interest Rates and Fee Structures

Hard money loans typically carry higher interest rates than conventional mortgages, often ranging between 10% and 15% annually in the Margate market. These higher rates compensate lenders for the increased risk and fast funding.

  • Origination fees: Usually 1-3% of the loan amount, paid upfront or rolled into the loan.
  • Points: Some lenders charge points (a percentage of the total loan) at closing.
  • Broker fees: Middlemen or brokers may add additional fees, so borrowers should clarify the full cost upfront.

"Understanding all associated costs beyond the interest rate helps investors accurately calculate project break-even points," notes Jane Smith, Senior Loan Officer at FlipFunding Pro.

Collateral and Security Interests

Hard money lenders secure their loans with a lien against the property itself, which means defaulting borrowers risk foreclosure.

  • First-position liens: Most often, lenders expect to hold the first mortgage position for maximum security.
  • Cross-collateralization: In some cases, lenders may require additional property as collateral for larger loan amounts.
  • Property insurance requirements: Borrowers generally must provide proof of adequate insurance coverage on the collateral property during the loan term.

This structure incentivizes borrowers to meet repayment deadlines while protecting lenders’ interests amid fluctuating market conditions.
