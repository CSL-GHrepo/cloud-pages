What Are Fix and Flip Loans?

Fix and flip loans, often categorized under real estate investment loans or hard money loans, are short-term financing products designed specifically for investors who purchase distressed properties, renovate them, and sell them quickly for a profit. Unlike traditional mortgages meant for homeowners or long-term investors, these loans focus on speed, flexibility, and funding property rehab costs.

  • Short-term duration: Typically 6 to 18 months, enough time to complete renovation and sale.
  • Higher interest rates: Rates often range between 8% and 14% due to increased risk and quick turnaround expectations.
  • Interest-only or deferred payments: Many fix and flip loans require interest payments only during the loan term, easing cash flow during rehab.
  • Faster approval process: Approval can take days to weeks, critical where property deals move fast.
  • Loan-to-value (LTV) ratio: Generally 65% to 75%, based on the property's after-repair value (ARV) rather than purchase price.

Many fix and flip financiers also provide property rehab funding as part of the loan, allowing investors to access funds incrementally based on renovation milestones.

Traditional Mortgages: A Brief Overview

Traditional mortgages, by contrast, are long-term loans designed primarily for purchase or refinance of owner-occupied homes or investment properties with the intention of long-term ownership.

  • Loan terms: Typically 15 to 30 years, with fixed or variable interest rates.
  • Lower interest rates: Often between 3% and 6%, depending on credit scores and market conditions.
  • Comprehensive underwriting: Detailed credit checks, income verification, and lengthy approval processes.
  • Loan-to-value (LTV): Usually up to 80% based on current property value, not ARV.
  • Less flexible use of funds: Mortgages do not commonly include rehab funding or other expenses beyond purchase price.

Traditional mortgages suit investors planning to hold properties for rental income or long-term appreciation, rather than quick resale.

Key Differences: Fix and Flip Loans vs. Traditional Mortgages

Understanding these key distinctions can help investors choose appropriate financing:

  1. Purpose and Usage
    Fix and flip loans focus on rapid turnover, with built-in funding for renovations, while traditional mortgages support longer-term investment or homeownership without rehab components.
  2. Loan Terms and Duration
    Fix and flip loans are short-term, typically under 18 months, versus 15-30 years for traditional mortgages.
  3. Interest Rates and Costs
    Fix and flip financing comes with higher rates and fees reflecting higher lender risk and shorter repayment periods.
  4. Approval Speed
    Hard money lenders offer rapid approvals, often within days, in contrast to the somber weeks or months of traditional underwriting.
  5. Collateral and Valuation
    Fix and flip loan amounts are calculated based on after-repair value (ARV), unlike purchase price or current appraised value for a traditional mortgage.
  6. Repayment Structure
    Many fix and flip loans provide interest-only payments enabling investors to allocate cash toward property rehab, while traditional mortgages require monthly principal and interest.

Why Choose Fix and Flip Financing?

For investors focused on house flipping, fix and flip loans are invaluable due to their structure and speed. Here’s why:

  • Access to rehab funds delivered in draws aligns financing with project needs, ensuring controlled spending.
  • Flexibility in underwriting allows candidates with lower credit scores or unconventional income sources to qualify.
  • Short loan terms reduce long-term financial exposure.

When Is a Traditional Mortgage a Better Fit?

In contrast, traditional mortgages suit investors who:

  • Plan to hold properties as rentals or long-term investments rather than quick sales.
  • Have strong credit profiles and income verification to secure low-interest rates.
  • Require predictable monthly payments over extended terms.
  • Do not need rehab or construction financing bundled.

While traditional mortgages can finance investment properties, the lack of property rehab funding means investors often must secure separate lines of credit or savings for renovations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main differences between fix and flip loans and traditional mortgages for investment properties?

Fix and flip loans are short-term, higher-interest loans designed to provide quick funds including rehab costs for properties intended to be sold quickly. Traditional mortgages have longer terms, lower interest rates, and are suited for long-term holdings without built-in rehab financing.

Can I use a traditional mortgage to finance a fix and flip project?

While possible, traditional mortgages are not ideal for fix and flip projects due to slower approval processes and lack of rehab funding. Investors usually prefer fix and flip loans for quicker access to capital and flexible draw disbursements during renovation.

What types of financing are considered fix and flip financing?

Fix and flip financing commonly includes hard money loans and other real estate investment loans specializing in short-term investment property loans with funding for purchase and rehab costs.

How do loan terms compare between fix and flip loans and traditional mortgages?

Fix and flip loans generally have terms from 6 to 18 months with interest rates between 8% to 14%, while traditional mortgages range from 15 to 30 years with rates typically between 3% and 6%.
