Understanding Loan-to-Value (LTV) and After Repair Value (ARV) in Hard Money Lending

Two critical components in hard money loans are loan-to-value (LTV) and after repair value (ARV)—terms crucial for investors to grasp when working with Flip Funding Pro or any hard money lender.

  • Loan-to-Value (LTV): This ratio describes the amount of the loan compared to the property’s current or projected value. In hard money loans, LTV is often based on ARV because it reflects the property’s value after renovations.
  • After Repair Value (ARV): The projected market value of a property after estimated repairs and renovations are complete. Flip Funding Pro uses ARV to determine loan eligibility and maximum amounts, helping investors finance both purchase and renovation costs.

For example, if a property’s ARV is estimated at $300,000 and Flip Funding Pro’s lending criteria allow an LTV of 70%, the maximum loan amount could be up to $210,000. This approach protects both the borrower and lender by aligning funding with the property's potential equity post-renovation.
