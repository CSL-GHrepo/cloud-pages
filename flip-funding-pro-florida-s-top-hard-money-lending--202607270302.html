Potential Risks of Hard Money Lending

  • Higher interest rates: Hard money loans typically carry higher annual percentage rates (APRs) compared to conventional bank financing due to the increased risk borne by lenders.
  • Shorter loan terms: Repayment periods are often compressed (6-24 months), which creates pressure for timely project completion and resale.
  • Loan-to-value constraints: The relatively conservative LTVs (usually capped around 75%) may require investors to bring substantial capital upfront.
  • Strict collateral focus: These loans are secured by property value, so project delays or market downturns affecting valuations can impact refinancing options and outcomes.
