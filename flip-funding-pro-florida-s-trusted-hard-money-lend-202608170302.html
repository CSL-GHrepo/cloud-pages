Understanding Loan-to-Value (LTV) and Its Impact on Your Investment

Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratios play a pivotal role in determining the amount of capital you can access for your project and the risk assessed by lenders. Flip Funding Pro’s typical LTV range of 65-75% reflects a carefully balanced approach tailored to Florida markets.

How LTV Affects Your Financing

  • Higher LTV Means More Capital: Borrowers can leverage a greater percentage of the property’s current or post-rehab value, which is helpful for maximizing rehab budgets.
  • Lower LTV Reduces Risk: A lower LTV signals less exposure for both lender and borrower, often resulting in better terms and less stringent conditions.
  • Post-Repair Value (ARV) Considerations: Flip Funding Pro often uses ARV to calculate LTV, allowing borrowers to finance based on anticipated property value after renovations.

Example: Calculating Loan Amount Based on LTV

Imagine you plan to flip a property with an estimated ARV of $200,000. At a 70% LTV, Flip Funding Pro might offer a loan up to $140,000. This figure would need to cover both purchase price and rehab costs, influencing your project budget planning and profit margins.

Understanding the implications of LTV empowers investors to better forecast financing capacity and associated risks. Flip Funding Pro’s transparent LTV approach lends confidence throughout project planning.
