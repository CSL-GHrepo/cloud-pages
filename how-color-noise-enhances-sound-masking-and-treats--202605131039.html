Quick Overview: What is Color Noise and Why Does it Matter?

Color noise refers to a range of noise types characterized by their specific frequency spectra — including white, pink, brown, and even blue or violet noise. Each color noise has a unique power distribution across frequencies, which influences how effectively it masks background noise or tinnitus.

Sound therapy devices utilize these noise color variations to customize sound masking, addressing individual auditory needs and improving masking effectiveness.

Understanding Applications of Color Noise in Sound Masking

Sound masking is the process of adding background noise to a sound environment to reduce the perception of unwanted sounds. Color noise is particularly effective for this due to its noise spectrum masking properties.

  • White Noise: Contains equal intensity across frequencies, similar to static; widely used but may feel harsh to some.
  • Pink Noise: Emphasizes lower frequencies, creating a softer, more natural sound often preferred for sound masking and relaxation.
  • Brown Noise: Emphasizes even lower frequencies, offering a deep, warm sound that some users find more soothing.

According to the Acoustic Society of America, pink and brown noise have higher masking effectiveness in certain environments by matching the natural hearing sensitivity curve more closely, making them ideal for use in sound therapy devices designed for tinnitus sufferers and workplace sound masking.
