In-Depth: Understanding Loan-to-Value Ratios and Their Impact on Your Deal

The Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio is a critical factor in hard money lending, especially in the fix-and-flip world. Flip Funding Pro typically offers LTVs between 65% and 75%, but understanding how this impacts your investment can inform better decision-making.

What is LTV and Why Does it Matter?

LTV is the percentage of the property's value that a lender is willing to finance. For example, a $300,000 property with a 70% LTV loan would qualify for a loan amount of $210,000.

  • Conservative vs. Aggressive LTV: Lower LTVs reduce lender risk but require more upfront capital from the borrower. Higher LTVs increase financing but carry more risk for both parties.
  • After Repair Value (ARV): Hard money lenders like Flip Funding Pro focus heavily on ARV — the estimated future value after renovations. This determines the maximum loan amount.
  • Investor’s Equity Cushion: A reasonable equity stake in the property encourages prudent budgeting and protects investors from market volatility.

Flip Funding Pro’s focus on realistic appraisals and market data ensures LTVs are structured to optimize returns while minimizing risk, a balance crucial in Florida’s diverse real estate environments.
