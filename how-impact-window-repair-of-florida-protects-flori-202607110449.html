Understanding the Technology Behind Impact Windows

Impact windows are the result of decades of innovation in glass and framing technology, designed specifically to prevent structural failure during extreme weather events. Their core technology hinges on laminated glass, a multi-layered construction that combines two pieces of glass with a tough interlayer made from polyvinyl butyral (PVB) or ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA). Unlike ordinary glass, which shatters upon impact, laminated glass holds together due to this internal layer, reducing the risk of dangerous shards flying into the home.

Beyond the glass itself, the framing systems used in impact windows are engineered to withstand uplift pressures from hurricane-force winds. Aluminum, fiberglass, and vinyl frames are commonly reinforced internally with steel or heavy-duty extrusions, increasing resistance to bending and warping.

Why Laminated Glass Outperforms Other Types

  • Strength and Durability: Laminated glass can absorb and dissipate impact energy without breaking apart, unlike tempered glass which breaks into small pieces.
  • Sound Reduction: The polymer interlayer dampens noise significantly, providing quieter interiors, a notable benefit for homes near busy roads or airports.
  • UV Protection: Laminated glass blocks over 99% of harmful UV rays, protecting interior furnishings from fading while improving occupant comfort.
  • Enhanced Security: The durable glass layer offers enhanced resistance to forced entry, adding a layer of security against burglaries beyond just wind protection.

Impact Window Repair of Florida’s technicians are trained to identify specific issues with laminated glass layers, such as delamination or deterioration of the interlayer, which can compromise both protection and clarity. Proper repair or replacement of affected panes is crucial to maintain these advanced benefits.

How Coastal Environments Affect Impact Window Longevity

Florida’s coastal areas present additional challenges for impact windows, primarily due to the salt-laden air that accelerates corrosion and degradation of window components.

  • Corrosion of Metal Frames and Hardware: Salt accelerates oxidation of aluminum frames and steel reinforcements, sometimes causing pitting or flaking that weakens structural integrity.
  • Sealant Breakdown: UV exposure combined with salt spray fatigues silicone and polyurethane sealants, leading to cracks or gaps which in turn enable moisture intrusion.
  • Glass Surface Etching: Prolonged salt deposits can cause micro-etching on glass surfaces which reduces clarity and can encourage fogging between panes.

Given these risks, routine cleaning with appropriate, non-abrasive products is critical in coastal homes. Professional maintenance services provided by companies like Impact Window Repair of Florida include cleaning regimens designed to remove salt residues safely and inspect all affected components for early signs of corrosion or wear.
