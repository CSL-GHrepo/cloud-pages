Understanding the Basics: What Are Pin Tumbler Locks?

The majority of residential and commercial locks are based on the pin tumbler lock design, invented over 150 years ago and still dominant today. These locks consist of a cylinder housing several small pins of varying lengths, which prevent the lock from turning unless the pins are aligned precisely at the shear line by the correct key.

  • Lower pins: These sit inside the plug cylinder and vary in length.
  • Upper pins: These rest inside the housing and are forced down by spring tension.
  • Shear line: The gap between the plug and the outer housing where the pins must line up perfectly to allow the cylinder to rotate.

Lock picking involves manipulating each pin, effectively mimicking the action of the correct key.

Step-By-Step Lockpicking Techniques Used by Locksmiths

1. Inserting and Applying Pressure with the Tension Wrench

The first lock picking tool a locksmith inserts is the tension wrench. This L-shaped piece of metal fits into the lock’s keyway and applies gentle rotational pressure in the direction the key would turn. Proper tension is crucial—too much and the pins won’t set, too little and pins fail to bind.

2. Using Lock Picks to Manually Manipulate the Pins

With the tension wrench gently applying torque, the locksmith inserts a pick—often a hook or rake shape—into the keyway to probe the pins one at a time. Pins are felt for resistance (binding). When a pin binds, it is pushed upward until it reaches the shear line and 'sets' in place.

Locksmiths demonstrate incredible skill through manual lock manipulation, carefully lifting each pin without oversetting any others. This precision is built over extensive locksmith training steps and continuous practice.

3. Recognizing Feedback Through Touch and Sound

Experienced locksmiths rely on tactile feedback and sometimes subtle auditory cues to know when a pin sets. This technique, sometimes called "listening to the lock," enhances accuracy during the process.

4. Repeat Until All Pins Are Set and the Lock Opens

Once all pins are aligned perfectly at the shear line, the plug can rotate freely, allowing the lock to open.

Lock Bypass Methods Beyond Traditional Lockpicking

While traditional pin manipulation is most common, locksmiths also employ various lock bypass methods depending on the lock’s design:

  • Shimming – Inserting thin metal inserts to manually lift locking latches, often used in padlocks.
  • Bumping – Using a "bump key" struck to momentarily force pins upward.
  • Impressioning – Creating a working key by molding an impression of the lock's pins.

However, many locks have advanced features to mitigate these techniques, highlighting the importance of the locksmith’s expertise and toolset.

Lock Picking Tools: What Do Locksmiths Use?

Professional locksmiths rely on a specific set of tools designed for various locking mechanisms:

  • Tension wrench: Applies rotational torque to the lock plug.
  • Hook picks: Target individual pins.
  • Rake picks: Used to rapidly manipulate multiple pins.
  • Diamond picks: Versatile for various pin layouts.
  • Plug spinners: Reverse plug rotation if the lock was turned in the wrong direction.

Each tool serves a dedicated purpose, and locksmiths select the appropriate ones based on the lock type and situation.

Lock Security Vulnerabilities Locksmiths Exploit

While locks aim to secure premises, their mechanical nature introduces known vulnerabilities that locksmiths understand intimately, such as:

  • Poor tolerances: Looser manufacturing can make pins easier to set.
  • Wear and tear: Older locks may have weakened springs or smoother pins.
  • Standardized pin layouts: Make some locks more predictable.

Industry studies show that over 90% of standard pin tumbler locks can be successfully picked with the right tools and skill (Source: The Open Organization of Lockpickers).

Frequently Asked Questions

What tools do locksmiths use to pick locks?

Locksmiths primarily use tension wrenches and various picks such as hook, rake, and diamond picks to manipulate the pins inside pin tumbler locks. These lock picking tools allow them to apply rotational pressure and manually set each pin at the shear line.

How long does it take a locksmith to pick a lock?

The time varies depending on the lock complexity and locksmith’s experience but typically ranges from a few seconds for simple locks to several minutes for high-security locks. Expert lock manipulation and proper tension wrench use speed up the process significantly.

Can all locks be picked by locksmiths?

Most standard pin tumbler locks can be picked by trained locksmiths, but high-security locks with anti-pick features or electronic locking mechanisms may require alternative bypass methods or specialized equipment.

Is lock picking legal and ethical?

Yes, when performed by licensed locksmiths for legitimate reasons such as lockouts, repairs, or security testing, lock picking is legal and ethical. Proper locksmith training steps emphasize adherence to laws and client consent to prevent misuse.
