Quick Answer: How Long Does a Fix and Flip Usually Take?

The total timeframe for completing a fix and flip project typically ranges from 90 to 180 days (3 to 6 months). This timeline covers everything from initial purchase and inspections to final renovations and resale preparations. Variables such as project size, contractor availability, permitting delays, and market conditions all influence the house flipping timeline.

According to a report by ATTOM Data Solutions, the average fix and flip project took about 6 months from purchase to sale in 2023, confirming this industry benchmark.[1]

Understanding the Fix and Flip Project Stages

Breaking down the fix and flip project stages helps set realistic expectations for timing:

  • Property Acquisition (1-4 weeks): Time to evaluate, finance, and close on a property.
  • Inspection and Planning (1-2 weeks): Comprehensive home inspections and creation of a detailed rehab plan.
  • Permitting and Approvals (2-6 weeks): Securing necessary local permits and approvals.
  • Demolition and Structural Rehab (2-4 weeks): Removing damaged materials, addressing foundational or framing issues.
  • Systems Upgrades (1-3 weeks): Electrical, plumbing, HVAC improvements or replacements.
  • Interior and Exterior Renovations (3-6 weeks): Installing drywall, flooring, painting, kitchens and baths remodels.
  • Final Inspection and Touch-Ups (1-2 weeks): Walkthroughs, minor corrections, and staging.
  • Market Listing and Sale (variable): Time required to list and sell the property post-renovation.

Each phase is interconnected. Delays in permitting or inspections can cascade and extend the overall property rehab schedule. Experienced flippers reduce downtime between stages through proactive project management.

Factors Affecting the Home Renovation Duration

The exact time frame depends heavily on:

  • Scope and Complexity of Work: A simple cosmetic flip versus a full gut rehab hugely changes the timeline. For example, replacing flooring and painting can take just weeks, while structural repairs or complete kitchen/bath remodels stretch the schedule.
  • Contractor Experience and Availability: Skilled contractors who specialize in flips tend to accelerate the remodeling time frame. Labor shortages or scheduling conflicts cause delays.
  • Supply Chain and Material Availability: In recent years, supply chain disruptions have impacted timelines, with certain flooring or fixtures on backorder by weeks.
  • Geographic Location and Local Regulations: Municipal permitting processes vary drastically. Some cities approve permits in a matter of days, others take more than a month.
  • Unexpected Issues Discovered Mid-Project: Hidden problems such as mold, pests, or structural damage can arise, leading to unplanned work.

Understanding these variables enables investors to build buffer time into their plans and avoid costly overruns.

Detailed Breakdown of Renovation Phases and Time Management Tips

Property Acquisition and Financing – Strategies to Speed Up Closing

The property acquisition phase often sets the pace for the entire fix and flip timeline. While some purchases close within a week, others can stretch out due to financing complexities or title issues. Here are strategies that seasoned investors use to accelerate this stage:

  • Pre-Approval for Financing: Getting mortgage or loan pre-approval before property hunting streamlines approvals and shortens lender processing times.
  • Cash Offers or Hard Money Loans: Using cash or hard money lenders significantly reduces lender contingencies and closing delays.
  • Work with Experienced Realtors: Realtors familiar with investment deals can help negotiate quicker contracts and navigate inspections efficiently.
  • Title and Escrow Preparation: Pre-ordering title searches and choosing escrow companies experienced with fix and flip transactions mitigates unexpected legal hold-ups.

Even small savings here—closing 10-14 days faster—can shave weeks off your overall timeline.

Inspection and Planning: Maximizing Efficiency and Accuracy

The inspection and planning stage is pivotal in identifying hidden repair needs and creating accurate renovation scope documents. Missing key issues at this phase can cause costly mid-project delays.

  • Hire Specialized Inspectors: Beyond general home inspectors, hire specialists for plumbing, electrical, roofing, and foundation where applicable.
  • Use Technology for Documentation: Apps and software facilitate real-time photo/video documentation and issue tracking, keeping all stakeholders informed.
  • Detailed Rehab Plans with Contingencies: Create layered scopes that prioritize critical repairs but include alternative plans for surprises.

Investors who spend an extra day or two confirming all renovation details tend to finish projects on or ahead of schedule with fewer unexpected revisions.

Demolition and Structural Work: Avoiding Common Pitfalls

As one of the most disruptive phases, demolition and structural repairs often determine whether downstream renovations proceed smoothly or face bottlenecks.

  • Early Permitting for Structural Changes: Failing to obtain permits before demo delays inspections and can incur fines.
  • Clear Debris Removal Plans: Schedule dumpsters and waste hauling upfront to prevent on-site clutter slowing progress.
  • Hire Structural Engineers When Needed: For foundation or framing issues, prompt evaluation by certified engineers ensures accurate repair methods, avoiding rework.

By anticipating challenges and sequencing work efficiently, flippers avoid costly hold-ups that add weeks to their renovation durations.

Systems Upgrades: Coordinating Trade Work for Optimal Flow

Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC sub-contractors often work sequentially but can overlap to accelerate a project—if coordinated properly.

  • Bundle Trade Work Smartly: Schedule plumbing rough-ins and electrical wiring in the same timeframe if site logistics allow.
  • Confirm Material Availability Early: Order specialty fixtures and equipment in advance to avoid trade delays waiting on backordered parts.
  • Flexible Scheduling Agreements: Negotiate with contractors for partial day or weekend work to compress timelines.
  • Routine Inspections During Upgrades: Arrange periodic city or third-party inspections to avoid re-inspection delays after all work is complete.

Coordination here is critical since system repairs are pre-requisites for drywall and finishes.

Interior and Exterior Renovations: Managing Multiple Trades and Deliveries

During the renovation’s core phase – painting, flooring, cabinetry, and exterior improvements – projects often involve multiple subcontractors, deliveries, and inspections happening simultaneously.

  • Use a Project Manager or General Contractor: A dedicated PM handles scheduling conflicts, material delivery timing, and quality control to keep work flowing smoothly.
  • Stagger Deliveries: Arrange for materials to arrive just-in-time rather than all at once to prevent space constraints and damage.
  • Implement Quality Checkpoints: Mid-phase walkthroughs catch defects early, avoiding rework close to project deadlines.
  • Weather-Proof Exteriors Early: Completing exterior repairs or painting early in the sequence helps protect newly installed interior finishes.

These practical tactics help avoid “last-minute scramble” crises that inflate timelines and budgets.

Final Inspection, Touch-Ups, and Staging: The Finishing Touches

Even after major renovations conclude, the project is not done until it is fully market-ready. Final walkthroughs and staging play a big role in ensuring a quick sale.

  • Plan for a 1-2 Week Contingency for Final Fixes: Allow buffer time for minor repairs and corrections after preliminary inspections.
  • Hire Professional Home Stagers: Well-staged homes attract buyers faster and often at higher prices.
  • Schedule Final City Inspections Early in the Process: Booking these ahead of completion dates prevents unplanned waits for municipal inspectors.

Preparation at this stage can significantly shorten time spent on market and improve resale timing.

Risk Management: Handling Unforeseen Delays and Budget Overruns

Common Unforeseen Issues and Their Timeline Impacts

Despite best planning, fix and flip projects frequently encounter surprises that disrupt timelines. Typical issues include:

  • Hidden Structural Damage: Undetected rot or termite damage extending demo and requiring urgent repairs.
  • Mold or Asbestos Remediation: Environmental hazards necessitating specialized abatement and testing phases.
  • Permit Rejections or Additional Requirements: Additional engineering reports, re-submissions delaying approvals.
  • Material Shortages or Price Spikes: Need to switch suppliers or delay ordering affecting subsequent phases.
  • Contractor No-Shows or Turnover: Having to find replacement crews adds onboarding time.

Projects can add anywhere from 2 weeks to several months depending on severity of these issues.

Building Contingency Time and Budget Into Your Project Plan

Successful investors treat fix and flip timelines as living documents, flexible to emerging risks rather than rigid deadlines. Approaches include:

  • Allocate at Least 10-20% Schedule Buffer: Planning for inevitable delays helps reduce stress and keeps downstream activities on track.
  • Contingency Budget of 10-15%: Having reserve funds for unexpected repairs or changes prevents funding issues that stop work.
  • Regular Risk Assessments: Weekly reassessments of project status, supply chain updates, and regulatory changes help catch potential disruptions early.
  • Clear Communication Protocols: Real-time updates between investors, contractors, and inspectors enable fast problem-solving.

Leveraging Technology to Shorten Your Fix and Flip Timeline

Technology tools have revolutionized real estate renovation project management, providing flippers with unprecedented control and oversight:

  • Project Management Software: Platforms like Buildertrend, CoConstruct, or Procore track schedules, budgets, and change orders all in one place with mobile access.
  • Digital Permitting Portals: Many municipalities offer online submission and tracking of permits, reducing back-and-forth visits.
  • Virtual Inspections: Some cities now allow video inspections via mobile devices, speeding approval processes.
  • Material Ordering and Inventory Apps: Tools integrate purchasing with workflow management, making materials ordering proactive instead of reactive.
  • Communication Platforms: Apps like Slack or Microsoft Teams streamline group messaging among all project vendors and stakeholders keeping information flowing.

Adopting these technologies helps professional flippers maintain momentum and minimize downtime.
