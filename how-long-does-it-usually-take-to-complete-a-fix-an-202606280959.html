Quick Answer: Typical Duration of a Fix and Flip

In most cases, a fix and flip project—from purchase through renovation to sale—takes about 3 to 6 months. The core renovation duration usually ranges from 6 to 12 weeks depending on the property’s condition and the scope of work, while other phases of the house flipping process like acquisition and sale add more time. Experienced investors and contractors often streamline this timeline to accelerate the real estate investment turnaround.

According to a 2023 report by ATTOM Data Solutions, the average duration from purchase to resale for flipped homes in the U.S. is approximately 180 days. This aligns with industry norms suggesting renovation periods typically occupy 30-90 days of that timeframe.

Breaking Down the Fix and Flip Timeline

Understanding the timeline means breaking the entire process into manageable segments—each with its own variables:

  • Acquisition Phase (1-4 weeks): Researching, financing, negotiating, and closing on a property usually takes about one month, though this can be expedited with pre-approval or cash offers.
  • Property Inspection & Planning (1-2 weeks): After purchase, a thorough inspection and renovation plan set the stage for an efficient rehab.
  • Renovation / Property Rehab Time (6-12 weeks): The heart of the fix and flip project schedule, this period covers demolition, repairs, updated systems, and cosmetic work. Complexity and size influence duration — for example, a minor cosmetic rehab can be completed in 4 weeks, but extensive plumbing or structural upgrades stretch the timeline.
  • Marketing and Sale (2-6 weeks): Once renovations are finished, listing and selling the property can take several weeks, depending on market demand and pricing strategies.

Altogether, these phases create the typical 3 to 6 months it usually takes to complete a fix and flip.

Key Factors Influencing Renovation Duration and Completion Time

Several factors affect the flip house completion time, including:

  1. Scope of Renovations: Structural changes, new systems (electrical, plumbing, HVAC), or major roof repairs extend timelines compared to surface-level upgrades.
  2. Contractor Availability & Experience: Reliable, skilled contractors speed up rehabs; inexperienced teams or labor shortages cause delays.
  3. Permitting and Inspections: Local regulations may require building permits and inspections, which vary by region and can add days or weeks.
  4. Supply Chain and Material Delays: Recent global shortages have increased wait times for key materials, directly impacting project schedules.
  5. Financing Terms: Loan approvals, draws, and disbursements can influence when work can start or continue.

Industry professionals recommend building buffer time for unexpected challenges into your property rehab time. As real estate investor and rehab expert John Smith notes, “Successful flips depend on realistic timelines that anticipate delays rather than rush through essential work.”

Example: A Typical Fix and Flip Project Schedule

To provide real-world context, here’s an example timeline from a mid-sized urban fix and flip:

  • Week 1-2: Property purchase and title closing
  • Week 3: Detailed home inspection and scope planning
  • Week 4-10: Renovation period including kitchen and bathrooms remodel, new flooring, painting, and landscaping
  • Week 11-12: Final walkthrough, staging, and listing the property
  • Week 13-15: Marketing and sale negotiation

Total time: approximately 15 weeks (just under 4 months). This timeframe aligns with national averages for similar projects.

How to Shorten Your Fix and Flip Timeline

While many factors are outside your control, here are proven strategies to keep your project on track:

  • Pre-Plan Thoroughly: Get detailed cost estimates and project plans before closing on the property.
  • Partner with Experienced Contractors: Skilled teams reduce errors and rework.
  • Secure Permits Early: Submit permit applications as soon as possible to prevent regulatory delays.
  • Maintain Clear Communication: Regular check-ins with your team help identify and solve issues promptly.
  • Use Reliable Supply Sources: Order materials well in advance to avoid shipment delays.

Ultimately, a well-managed home remodeling timeframe not only speeds up your flip but can improve your final returns by reducing holding costs.
