Understanding Copier Buyout Options at Lease End

Copier lease agreements typically offer three basic end-of-lease options:

  • Return the Copier: You send the machine back to the leasing company. Beware of potential end of lease copier fees for excess wear, damage, or usage overages.
  • Renew or Extend the Lease: Sometimes leasing companies allow you to continue monthly payments under revised terms.
  • Purchase the Copier (Lease Buyout): You buy the copier outright, often at a price predetermined in the contract—also called the lease buyout price.

Choosing to purchase the copier is ideal if your current machine meets your needs and buying it is more economically sensible than acquiring new equipment. You maintain control of the asset, avoid disruption, and may save money long term.

How Is the Lease Buyout Price Calculated?

The lease buyout price calculation hinges primarily on the copier residual value, agreed to before the lease begins. Residual value represents the machine’s estimated worth at the lease’s end, typically expressed as a percentage of the original cost.

  • Original Equipment Cost: What the copier would have cost new at lease inception.
  • Residual Value Percentage: Typically 10-50%, depending on lease length and machine type.
  • Residual Value Dollar Amount: Original cost multiplied by the residual percentage.

For example, if your copier originally cost $10,000 and the residual value is 20%, the buyout price may be around $2,000, excluding taxes or fees.

Additional fees can include:

  • Applicable sales tax on the purchase price.
  • Processing or administrative fees for lease termination.
  • Outstanding lease payments or charges for excess usage, if applicable.

Steps to Purchasing Your Leased Copier

To successfully complete the copier lease end purchase, follow these steps:

  1. Review Your Lease Agreement Early: Understand your contract’s buyout price, residual value clause, and any end of lease copier fees.
  2. Request a Buyout Quote: Contact your leasing company or vendor to get an updated buyout price, including any fees or taxes.
  3. Inspect the Copier: Evaluate the copier’s condition, print volume, and whether it fits your ongoing business needs.
  4. Negotiate if Possible: Some leasing companies may be flexible on price or fees.
  5. Secure Financing if Needed: If purchasing outright is a cash flow concern, consider leasing the buyout amount or arranging a loan.
  6. Finalize the Purchase: Sign the required buyout paperwork and ensure title transfer for copier ownership after lease.

Remember, after purchasing a leased copier, you become the owner and assume responsibility for maintenance, repairs, and consumables. This may open opportunities to reduce costs by choosing your own service providers.

  • Administrative or termination processing fees
