Understanding Your Fix and Flip Financing Options

Whether you’re a first-time investor or an experienced flipper, choosing the right financing vehicle influences your overall success. Here’s a breakdown of the top funding options used in fix and flip deals:

1. Hard Money Loans

Hard money loans are short-term loans from private investors or companies that base approval mostly on the property’s value rather than borrower creditworthiness.

  • Speed: Approval and funding can occur within days, ideal for competitive markets.
  • Loan-to-Value (LTV): Typically 65-75%, based on the ARV (After Repair Value).
  • Interest Rates and Fees: Higher than traditional loans — usually 8-15% interest with origination fees between 2-5%.

Hard money lenders focus on the property and exit strategy, making these loans the go-to for quick acquisitions and rehab financing.

2. Private Lenders

Private lenders are individuals or small groups providing personal capital for fix and flip deals.

  • Flexibility: Terms can be negotiated, including interest rates, repayment schedule, and loan structure.
  • Personal Relationships: Can be easier to secure with a good track record or local networking.
  • Speed: Generally faster than banks but slower than hard money loans depending on the lender.

Private lending often suits repeat investors who value long-term partnerships and customized financing.

3. Bridge Loans

Bridge loans act as temporary financing to cover gaps when transitioning between loans or selling.

  • Use Case: Fund the purchase before permanent financing or sale.
  • Terms: Short duration (6-12 months), relatively high-interest rates.
  • Collateral: Usually the property itself.

Bridge loans are effective when quick closing is needed, but the long-term plan involves refinancing or resale.

4. Construction Loans

Construction loans are designed for new builds or full rehabs with staged disbursement based on project milestones.

  • Draw Schedule: Funds are released as work completes, ensuring accountability.
  • Interest: Paid only on funds disbursed to date.
  • Complexity: More paperwork and oversight than hard money or private loans.

Though less common for typical flips, construction loans suit investors tackling large-scale rehabs or multiple units.

5. Fix and Flip Mortgages

Traditional mortgages aren’t built for fix and flip investors but some lenders offer specialized fix and flip mortgage programs.

  • Qualifying: Usually requires better credit and larger down payments than other options.
  • Terms: Can extend longer, offering better interest rates, but slower approval.
  • When to Use: Investors with strong financials and a longer project timeline.

How to Choose the Best Financing Option for Your Fix and Flip

  • Project timeline: Hard money and bridge loans work best for tight deadlines.

  • Loan purpose: Full rehab loans require construction-style disbursements.
  • Exit strategy: Your plan for resale or refinance impacts lender willingness.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common fix and flip financing options?

The most common options include hard money loans, private lenders, bridge loans, construction loans, and specialized fix and flip mortgages. Hard money loans are favored for their quick approval and property-based underwriting, while private lenders offer flexible, relationship-based funding. Bridge loans help cover short-term financing gaps, and construction loans support rehab projects with staged draws. Fix and flip mortgages, though less common, suit investors with strong financials and longer timelines.

How do hard money loans work for fix and flip deals?

Hard money loans provide fast, short-term financing secured by the property’s value instead of the borrower’s credit. They typically cover 65-75% of the purchase or ARV and have higher interest rates (8-15%) with upfront fees. These loans are ideal for fix and flip investors needing quick capital to close deals and finance renovations, with repayment often planned upon resale.

Can I use a traditional mortgage to finance a fix and flip?

Traditional mortgages are not typically designed for fix and flip projects because they require longer approval times and have stricter underwriting criteria regarding credit and occupancy. However, some lenders offer fix and flip mortgages tailored to investment properties, which often demand higher down payments and better credit but can offer lower interest rates and longer terms for those meeting the qualifications.

What factors should influence my choice of fix and flip financing?

Key factors include the speed at which you need funding, your creditworthiness, the scope and budget of the rehab, your exit strategy, and your familiarity with lenders. Projects with tight deadlines benefit from fast-closing hard money or bridge loans, while longer projects with sound financials may afford fix and flip mortgages. Additionally, local market conditions and lender availability also impact your best financing choice.
