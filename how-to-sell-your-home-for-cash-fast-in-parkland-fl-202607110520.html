How Selling for Cash Impacts Your Tax Situation in Florida

Sellers often overlook how a quick cash sale can affect their taxes. While Parkland does not levy state income tax, federal implications may apply depending on your circumstances.

Capital Gains Tax Considerations

If you sell your primary residence, you may qualify for exclusions on capital gains tax — up to $250,000 for individuals or $500,000 for married couples filing jointly, provided you meet ownership and use conditions. Quick cash sales follow the same tax rules as traditional sales:

  • Calculate gain as the difference between selling price and your adjusted basis (purchase price plus improvements).
  • Report the sale on your federal tax return if you don’t qualify for an exclusion.

Tax Reporting and Documentation

  • Ensure you keep detailed records of the transaction, including contracts, closing statements, and proof of improvements.
  • Report proceeds to the IRS as required, typically on Form 1099-S issued by the closing title company.

Consult a Tax Professional

Because every seller’s situation is unique, engaging a qualified CPA or tax advisor familiar with Florida real estate can help you understand any specific liabilities or deductions you may have related to a cash sale.
