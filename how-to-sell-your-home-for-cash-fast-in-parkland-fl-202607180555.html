Understanding Legal and Tax Implications of Selling for Cash in Parkland, FL

Selling your home for cash has its conveniences, but it’s important to be fully aware of any legal and tax responsibilities to avoid surprises later.

Capital Gains Tax Considerations

If the property you’re selling has appreciated in value since purchase, you may be subject to capital gains tax, even on a cash sale. However, the IRS provides exclusions if the home was your primary residence:

  • Single filers: Can exclude up to $250,000 of capital gains.
  • Married filed jointly: Can exclude up to $500,000.

To qualify, the home must have been your primary residence for at least two of the previous five years. Non-primary residences or investment properties are generally taxable, so consult a tax professional to understand your specific situation.

Disclosure Obligations Specific to Florida Law

Florida requires sellers to disclose certain known deficiencies or defects in the property. Failure to comply can lead to legal consequences or liability claims after the sale.

  • Structural issues including foundation and roof defects
  • Presence of mold, asbestos, or other hazardous materials
  • Past damage from flooding or hurricanes
  • Known neighborhood nuisances or environmental hazards

In cash sales, these disclosures are equally important despite the typically “as-is” nature of the transaction. Being upfront safeguards you legally and builds buyer trust that could speed the deal.

Engage a Qualified Real Estate Attorney
