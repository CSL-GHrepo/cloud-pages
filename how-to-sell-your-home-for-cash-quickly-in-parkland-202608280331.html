Understanding Tax Implications When Selling Your Home for Cash in Parkland

Selling your home for cash can have tax consequences that deserve careful consideration, particularly if you realize significant gains or sell an investment property.

Capital Gains Tax on Primary Residences

If your Parkland home was your primary residence and you meet IRS criteria (ownership and use test for two out of the last five years), you may exclude up to $250,000 of capital gains ($500,000 for married couples) when selling.

  • Considerations: Selling for cash doesn’t change your tax liabilities compared to traditional sales, but it is critical to report the sale properly in your tax filings.
  • Advice: Consult with a tax professional to understand your specific situation and take advantage of any exclusions or deductions.

Selling Investment or Rental Properties

Properties that are not primary residences, such as rentals or vacation homes, are subject to different capital gains tax rules and potentially depreciation recapture taxes.

  • Opportunity: Some sellers use 1031 exchanges to defer capital gains by reinvesting proceeds in like-kind properties.
  • Note: A cash sale qualifies for this exchange if structured properly but consult a qualified intermediary early in the process to comply with IRS regulations.

State and Local Taxes

Florida does not have a state income tax, which generally benefits home sellers. However, you should be mindful of:

  • Documentary stamp taxes: Usually paid by the seller or buyer on the deed transfer in Broward County, where Parkland is located.
  • Property tax prorations: Taxes owed or credited on closing to balance obligations.
“Complete understanding of tax responsibilities reduces surprises at closing—sellers should budget accordingly for document stamp fees and prorated taxes in Broward,” advises CPA Karen Li specializing in Florida real estate taxation.
