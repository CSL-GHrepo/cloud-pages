Tax Considerations for Cash Home Sales in Nevada

Sellers should also consider potential tax implications when selling their homes, regardless of whether the transaction is cash or financed. In Nevada, there is no state income tax, which can simplify the tax picture.

  • Capital Gains Exclusion: If the property was your primary residence for at least two of the last five years, you may qualify to exclude up to $250,000 of capital gains if single, or $500,000 if married filing jointly.
  • Inherited Property Sales: If you’re selling an inherited home, understand the “step-up” basis rule, which often adjusts the property's value to its fair market price at the time of inheritance. This can reduce taxable gains.
  • Reporting Cash Sales: All sales transactions must be reported to the IRS, regardless of payment method. Cash transactions over $10,000 are typically reported to the IRS via Form 8300 to prevent money laundering or tax evasion. However, selling a legitimate property is a standard, reportable event.

Before proceeding, it’s wise to consult with a tax professional experienced with Nevada real estate to fully understand implications based on your unique situation.
