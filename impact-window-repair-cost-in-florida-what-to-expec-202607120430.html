Materials Used in Impact Window Repairs: Quality vs. Cost

The choice of materials used in impact window repair significantly affects both the quality and cost of the service. Understanding the options available can help Florida homeowners make informed decisions aligned with their budget and durability needs.

Types of Glass for Impact Window Repair

  • Laminated Glass: The most common in impact windows, laminated glass consists of two or more layers of glass with an interlayer of polyvinyl butyral (PVB) or ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA). The interlayer holds the glass together if shattered, offering superior wind and debris resistance.
  • Tempered Glass: While stronger than regular glass, tempered glass alone is less effective than laminated glass during hurricane impact and typically not used alone in impact windows. Repairs involving tempered glass are generally less expensive but may require full window unit replacement for code compliance.
  • Insulated Glass Units (IGUs): For added energy efficiency, some impact windows include IGUs that have multiple panes separated by air or gas-filled spaces. Repairs of IGUs with impact resistance features can increase cost due to the complexity of maintaining seal integrity and thermal properties.

Frame Materials and Their Repair Implications

  • Vinyl Frames: Affordable and low maintenance, vinyl typically offers a good balance of price and durability. Repairing vinyl frames usually involves patching or replacing segments, which is cost-effective but limited by material flexibility.
  • Aluminum Frames: Known for strength and corrosion resistance, aluminum frames require specialized labor for realignment, dent removal, or repainting. Repairs tend to be costlier than vinyl but offer increased longevity in Florida’s humid climate.
  • Wood Frames: Wood frames provide aesthetic appeal but are highly vulnerable to Florida weather damage such as rot and warping. Repairing wood frames entails re-staining, sealant application, or component replacement and usually commands the highest labor and material costs.

Choosing the right material for repairs should factor in both immediate repair cost and projected lifespan extension.
