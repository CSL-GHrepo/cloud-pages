Quick Overview: What Is Rekeying?

Rekeying a lock involves altering the lock’s internal pins and tumblers so that it can be operated by a new key, rendering any old keys useless. It’s a precise, professional service performed by a locksmith that maintains the existing hardware while changing the key configuration.

“Rekeying locks is a practical solution for enhancing security without the cost and hassle of changing door hardware entirely,” says Jason Miller, certified locksmith with 15 years of experience.

Lock Rekeying Benefits vs Lock Replacement

When deciding between lock replacement vs rekeying, several factors come into play, including cost, security needs, and lock condition. Here’s how the two options stack up:

  • Cost: Rekeying typically costs between $20 to $50 per lock, whereas replacing a lock can cost $100 or more, depending on the lock type and complexity.
  • Time: Rekeying is a faster process, often completed within 30 minutes for residential locks, while replacements can take longer due to hardware selection and installation.
  • Security: Rekeying offers immediate security by invalidating lost or stolen keys. Replacement is necessary only if the lock is damaged or outdated.
  • Hardware Retention: Rekeying keeps your existing lock hardware, beneficial if you’re satisfied with the lock’s appearance and condition.

Industry studies show that over 60% of homeowners choose rekeying as a cost-effective alternative to replacing locks after moving into a new home. (Source: Home Security Research Institute, 2023)

Security Advantages of Rekeying

Many people overlook one key aspect: the security benefit of rekeying. By reprogramming the lock to a new key, any lost, stolen, or unauthorized keys are instantly nullified — providing peace of mind for homeowners, renters, and business operators alike.

“If your keys are ever misplaced or if you change tenants frequently, rekeying is your first line of defense against unauthorized access,” notes locksmith expert Sarah Lee.

When Should You Replace Locks Instead?

Rekeying is not always the best choice. Situations that justify lock replacement include:

  • Worn or Damaged Locks: Locks that are old, corroded, or malfunctioning may not respond well to rekeying and should be replaced.
  • Upgrading Security: Replacing locks can be a smart investment if you want to switch to high-security or smart lock systems.
  • Lost Master Keys or Security Breach: In complex commercial systems with master keys, replacement ensures the entire system is secure.

“The decision to replace a lock often comes down to the condition of the lock and specific security needs,” advises Jason Miller.

The Cost of Rekeying Locks vs Replacement

Cost remains a primary consideration for anyone seeking locksmith services. Here’s a breakdown:

  1. Rekeying Locks: $20-$50 per lock.
  2. Lock Replacement: $100-$300+ per lock, depending on style and technology.
  3. Additional Services: Rekeying often avoids the need for new keys cuts, door modifications, or hardware upgrades.

If you have multiple locks, consider that rekeying all locks to work with a single key (keying alike) is a common service locksmiths provide to simplify access.

Lock Maintenance Tips to Prolong Security

Whether you choose rekeying or replacement, maintaining your locks enhances durability and safety:

  • Regularly lubricate locks using graphite or silicone-based spray (avoid oil lubricants).
  • Test locks periodically to ensure smooth operation.
  • Immediately address any signs of wear or difficulty locking/unlocking.
  • Consult with your locksmith if you experience strange noises or resistance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it always possible to rekey a lock instead of replacing it?

Most standard cylinder locks can be rekeyed by a professional locksmith; however, rekeying is not possible if the lock is damaged, very old, or a non-standard type like tubular or high-security integrated electronic locks. In such cases, replacement is recommended.

What are the main security advantages of lock rekeying?

Rekeying renders lost or stolen keys useless by changing the lock’s internal mechanism, providing immediate security. It eliminates risks associated with duplicate keys held by former tenants, employees, or previous owners without the cost of hardware replacement.

How much does it typically cost to rekey locks compared to replacing them?

The cost of rekeying locks generally ranges from $20 to $50 per lock, making it significantly less expensive than lock replacement, which can range from $100 to $300 or more depending on the type of lock and additional hardware requirements.

When should I consider replacing my locks instead of rekeying?

Consider lock replacement if your locks show signs of wear, malfunction, or damage, or if you want to upgrade to high-security or electronic locks. Also, replacement may be necessary if your lock system requires master key changes or after a security breach involving lock tampering.
