The Financial Comparison: Buying vs Leasing Copier Costs

Understanding the financial impact is crucial when choosing between leasing and buying a copier. Here’s how they differ:

  • Upfront Cost: Buying requires a significant initial investment which can strain cash flow, often ranging from $3,000 to $15,000 or more depending on the model. Leasing spreads these costs over time with manageable monthly payments, sometimes as low as $100-$300 per month.
  • Total Cost of Ownership: While purchasing may be cheaper over the long haul, this does not always factor in ongoing maintenance, consumables, and eventual replacement costs. Leasing agreements usually include copier maintenance agreements, which cover repairs and parts, reducing unexpected expenses.
  • Tax Implications: Lease payments are typically deductible as business operating expenses, whereas purchased equipment may require depreciation over years.

Copier Leasing Advantages: Why More Businesses Are Choosing to Lease

Copier leasing has become increasingly popular as a smart business decision. Here are some compelling copier leasing advantages to consider:

  • Lower Initial Investment: Leasing frees up working capital for other business needs rather than tying it down in equipment.

  • Maintenance Included: Most commercial copier lease terms include maintenance agreements, meaning the leasing company manages repairs, toner replacement, and servicing.
  • Predictable Monthly Expenses: Leasing helps in budgeting by providing fixed monthly costs, reducing financial surprises.
  • Flexible Terms and End-of-Lease Options: Businesses can choose to purchase the copier at lease end, upgrade to new models, or simply return the equipment, offering flexibility aligned with changing needs.

Business Copier Financing Options: Exploring Your Choices

If you lean toward ownership but want manageable payments, financing through loans or payment plans is another route. Here’s what to consider:

  • Loans: Typically require credit checks and collateral. You own the copier immediately but must handle maintenance and repairs.
  • Payment Plans: Some vendors offer zero-interest or low-interest payment plans, balancing ownership with smaller immediate costs.
  • Leasing: Technically avoids the debt on your balance sheet and bundles maintenance and upgrades.

Each financing option has benefits and risks. Leasing remains attractive because it combines financing with service and flexibility; however, purchasing can provide more control and long-term savings if your business can handle maintenance and technological obsolescence.
