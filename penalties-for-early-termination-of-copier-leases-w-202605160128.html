Understanding Copier Lease Agreements and Early Termination Fees

Copier lease agreements are legally binding contracts that specify the rental period, monthly payments, and obligations of both parties. Early termination fees are penalties charged when the lessee ends the lease before its agreed-upon expiration.

Common components of these penalties include:

  • Early Termination Fee: A fee calculated as a percentage of remaining lease payments or a flat sum set forth in the lease.
  • Lease Buyout Options: Some contracts allow you to purchase the copier outright by paying a predetermined amount, avoiding ongoing penalties.
  • Lease Exit Fees: Additional charges that may cover administrative costs related to terminating the lease.

According to NFIB, businesses that terminate copier leases prematurely often face penalties averaging 20-30% of the total lease value.

Why Do Copier Lease Companies Charge Early Termination Penalties?

Leasing companies invest in the equipment upfront and expect returns through consistent lease payments over the contract term. Early termination interrupts this revenue stream and can leave them with leased assets that may depreciate or be hard to remarket.

"Early termination fees incentivize lessees to fulfill their contracts and help leasing companies recover costs on used equipment," explains Mark Reynolds, a senior leasing analyst.

In addition, copier rental contracts often cover services like maintenance and support calculated over the lease term to balance costs. Canceling early can disrupt these plans, requiring compensation via lease cancellation penalties.
