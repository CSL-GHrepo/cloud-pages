Common Residential Garage Door Sizes

Residential garage doors typically follow a set of standard widths and heights designed to accommodate most passenger vehicles and standard garage spaces.

  • Width options: 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, and 16 feet are the most common garage door widths available.
  • Height options: Standard garage door height is mostly 7 feet, with an increasing number of homes opting for 8-foot tall doors to accommodate larger vehicles like trucks and SUVs.

Single-car garages generally use doors between 8 to 10 feet wide, while double-car garage doors are commonly 16 feet wide. For example, a standard single garage door size is often 9 feet wide by 7 feet tall.

According to industry data from the Door & Access Systems Manufacturers Association (DASMA), 85% of residential garage doors fall within these standard size ranges, facilitating easier replacement and maintenance.

Experts recommend measuring your current garage door opening, including the width, height, and depth of the space, before ordering a new door. “For most homeowners, a door that’s 16x7 feet covers the majority of residential needs, balancing space and cost effectively,” says John Miller, a garage door installation specialist with over 15 years’ experience.

Commercial Garage Door Dimensions

Commercial garage doors vary greatly due to the different needs across industries, but common sizes can provide a baseline:

  • Widths: Commercial doors often range from 10 to 20 feet wide, with custom sizes exceeding 25 feet for warehouses or vehicle service centers.
  • Heights: Commercial garage door heights typically range from 12 to 16 feet, accommodating larger vehicles such as delivery trucks and heavy equipment.

Oversized garage doors are tailor-made for special vehicles, accommodating everything from RVs to large commercial trucks. When considering a commercial or oversized garage door, precise measurement of your garage door opening dimensions is critical to ensure safe and efficient operation.

Industry studies indicate commercial garage door installation projects report 30% fewer delays when clients provide detailed opening measurements upfront (accredited studies by the International Door Association).

Custom and Oversized Garage Door Sizing

When standard sizes won’t fit your garage or specific needs, custom garage door sizing becomes essential. Here’s what you need to know:

  1. Detailed measurements: Measure the width and height of your garage door opening, including any trim or moldings.
  2. Consider structural aspects: Pay attention to the wall header height and the backroom depth—the space behind the opening that allows the door to open properly.
  3. Vehicle dimensions: Account for your vehicle size to ensure comfortable clearance.
  4. Special features: Custom doors can include windows, insulation, or unique materials suited to your design preferences.

For example, a customer with an RV might require a garage door measuring 14 feet wide by 14 feet tall. Custom sizing requires partnering with experienced garage door professionals familiar with non-standard dimensions and local building codes.

Key Tips for Measuring Garage Door Dimensions

Accurate garage door measurements prevent costly mistakes. Follow these guidelines:

  • Width Measurement: Measure the inside width of the door frame from left to right.
  • Height Measurement: Measure from the floor to the top of the door opening.
  • Headroom: Measure the distance from the top of the door opening to the ceiling or obstruction.
  • Sideroom: Measure the space available on both sides of the garage door opening.
  • Backroom Depth: The distance from the garage door opening inside the garage to the wall opposite the door.

Having these dimensions clearly documented helps installers recommend the perfect garage door system and opener mechanism.

“Understanding your garage door’s exact dimensions is fundamental. Even a few inches off can affect the door's functionality and safety,” notes Sarah Jones, a garage door engineer specializing in custom installations.

Regional Considerations & Final Thoughts

While garage door sizes are largely universal, keep regional factors in mind. For example, areas with heavy snowfall may benefit from insulated doors or slightly higher clearances for snow removal equipment. Regions with hurricane codes or extreme weather will require additional reinforcement, which may impact door thickness and installation methods.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common garage door widths for residential properties?

The most common residential garage door widths include 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, and 16 feet. Single-car garage doors typically fall between 8 and 10 feet wide, while double-car doors are usually 16 feet wide. These standard widths accommodate the majority of passenger vehicles and allow for safe clearance.

What is the standard garage door height?

Standard garage door heights are generally 7 or 8 feet tall. While 7 feet has been the traditional height for many decades, 8-foot tall doors are becoming increasingly popular, especially for homes with larger vehicles like trucks and SUVs.

How do commercial garage door dimensions differ from residential sizes?

Commercial garage doors are larger to accommodate heavy equipment and vehicles. They commonly start at 10 feet wide and 12 feet tall but can go up to 20 feet wide or more. Several industries require oversized doors with even greater height and width, tailored to their specific vehicle clearance needs.

How can I accurately measure my garage door opening dimensions for a replacement?

Measure the width from inside left to inside right of the door frame, and the height from the floor to the top of the opening. Additionally, measure the headroom (space above the door), sideroom (space on both sides), and backroom (distance inside the garage). These measurements ensure proper fit and functional installation.
