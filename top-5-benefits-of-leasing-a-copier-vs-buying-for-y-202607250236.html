Leasing provides potential tax benefits and simplifies accounting processes. Lease payments are generally considered operating expenses and can be fully deducted on business tax returns, unlike capital expenditures which require depreciation over time.

This distinction can improve a business’s taxable income position and improve cash flow. Additionally, keeping leased equipment off the balance sheet can enhance financial ratios and borrowing capacity.

According to the Equipment Leasing and Finance Association, nearly 90% of financial advisors recommend leasing as a strategic tool to maximize tax advantages and manage assets effectively.

Common Myths About Copier Leasing Debunked

Despite its advantages, misconceptions about copier leasing still persist. Addressing these myths can help businesses make better-informed decisions.

Myth 1: Leasing is More Expensive Than Buying

Reality: While monthly payments add up, leasing often reduces total cost of ownership by spreading finances, including service and upgrades, avoiding costly repairs, and preserving working capital for other investments.

Myth 2: Leasing Contracts Are Rigid and Hard to Change

Reality: Many leasing companies offer flexible contracts with options to scale, upgrade, or modify terms based on business needs, especially with modern leasing models.

Myth 3: You Don’t Own the Equipment, So It’s Not Worth It

Reality: Ownership isn’t always the goal—access to latest technology, predictable expenses, and maintenance coverage can provide greater overall value than possession alone.

Myth 4: Maintenance Plans Are Too Restrictive

Reality: Maintenance services included with leases typically enhance equipment uptime and reduce unexpected costs, often customized to avoid disruption during critical periods.

Understanding these realities helps businesses evaluate leasing based on actual benefits rather than outdated perceptions.
