Traditional home sales usually involve various costs that can reduce the seller’s net proceeds:

  • Extended Holding Costs: Mortgage payments, utilities, and insurance during listing period.

By contrast, selling to a cash buyer often eliminates or reduces many of these expenses, which can quickly add up to thousands of dollars saved. For example, a Parkland homeowner who sold to a cash buyer saved over $15,000 in agent commissions and repairs, closing the sale in just 10 days.

Tax Considerations When Selling a Home for Cash

Regardless of how the sale is transacted—cash or financed—tax rules apply the same. Sellers should be aware of capital gains tax implications, especially if the home is not the primary residence or if selling a rental property.

  • Primary Residence Exclusion: Homeowners selling their primary residence may exclude up to $250,000 ($500,000 for married couples) of capital gains, provided they meet the IRS ownership and use tests.
  • Investment Properties: Capital gains taxes often apply, but 1031 exchanges might be an option to defer taxes when reinvesting proceeds.

Consulting a tax professional is advisable to understand specific financial outcomes and strategies when selling to cash buyers, especially if the transaction is motivated by urgent financial concerns or inheritance.
