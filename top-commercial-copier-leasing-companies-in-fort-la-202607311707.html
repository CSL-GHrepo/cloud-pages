Cost Analysis: Leasing vs. Buying Copiers for Fort Lauderdale Businesses

While leasing is increasingly popular, some Fort Lauderdale businesses still weigh the option of outright purchasing copiers. Understanding the financial and operational implications of both can guide better decisions.

Initial Capital Outlay

Leasing copiers involves minimal upfront costs, often limited to a security deposit or first month’s payment. Buying requires significant capital investment which can strain cash flow for growing companies.

Technology Refresh and Obsolescence

Technological advancements in copiers happen frequently. Leasing enables:

  • Regular technology refresh cycles that keep businesses equipped with the latest features.
  • Reduced risk of owning outdated equipment that may become costly to maintain or replace.

Buying locks the firm into the purchased model, limiting flexibility.

Maintenance and Unexpected Expenses

Leased devices typically come with inclusive maintenance plans reducing surprise repair costs. Owners of purchased units are fully responsible for servicing, parts replacement, and consumables.

Balance Sheet and Tax Considerations

Leasing payments are generally recorded as operational expenses, potentially offering tax advantages and improving financial ratios. Purchased assets must be capitalized and depreciated over time, affecting accounting differently.
