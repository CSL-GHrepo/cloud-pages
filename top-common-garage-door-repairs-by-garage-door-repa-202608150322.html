Common Garage Door Safety Inspections and Why They Matter

Safety must always be a top priority when dealing with garage doors, which can be heavy, moving at high speeds, and powered by strong springs under tension. Many accidents and injuries happen due to overlooked safety issues, which is why professional safety inspections are vital.

What to Expect During a Comprehensive Safety Inspection

A Garage Door Repair of Plantation, FL safety inspection typically covers:

  • Balance Testing: Evaluates whether the door stays in place when partially opened, an indicator of spring condition and cable integrity.
  • Auto-Reverse Sensor Check: Ensures sensors detect obstacles and reverse the door’s direction promptly to prevent injury or property damage.
  • Hardware and Fastener Tightening: Identifies loose bolts, nuts, and screws that can cause misalignment or sudden failure.
  • Spring and Cable Wear Assessment: Detects cracks, rust, or frays that signal impending breakdowns.
  • Opener Safety Features Test: Verifies emergency release cords, force settings, and manual operation in case of power failure.

“One small overlooked issue can turn into a serious safety hazard; regular professional inspections not only protect your family but preserve the longevity of your garage door system,” emphasizes Rick Myers, senior technician.

Plantation residents can schedule inspections based on manufacturer recommendations or at least once annually to ensure safe, dependable operation.

DIY Garage Door Safety Tips Between Professional Inspections

While professional inspections are essential, there are basic safety checks homeowners can perform regularly:

  • Visual Inspection: Look for obvious signs of damage such as bent tracks, frayed cables, or worn rollers.
  • Manual Test: Disconnect the opener and manually lift the door halfway—if it stays in place, the springs are likely balanced.
  • Sensor Function: Clean photo-eye sensors and test reversal by placing an object in the door’s path.
  • Lubrication: Apply silicone-based lubricant to rollers, springs, and hinges—avoiding grease that can attract dust.
  • Keep Children Clear: Educate children about the dangers of playing near or with garage doors and remotes.

These actions can prevent emergencies and complement scheduled Plantation garage door maintenance.
