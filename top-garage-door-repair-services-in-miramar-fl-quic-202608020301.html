Understanding Garage Door Spring Types and Their Specific Repair Needs

Garage door springs are integral to counterbalancing door weight, yet different types require distinct handling and repair techniques. Knowing these distinctions helps clarify the expertise Garage Door Repair of Miramar brings to every job.

Torsion Springs vs. Extension Springs

  • Torsion Springs: Mounted above the door opening on a metal shaft, torsion springs provide consistent tension and are generally safer and longer-lasting but expensive.
  • Extension Springs: Attach to the sides of the door tracks and stretch when the door closes, often used in lighter doors.

Replacement of torsion springs involves calibrated winding and precise mounting to prevent injury or door malfunction. Extension springs require specialized containment cables to prevent accidents if the spring snaps.

Signs Indicating Spring Replacement

  • Door fails to stay open or is extremely hard to lift manually.
  • Visible gaps or breaks in the spring coils.
  • Loud banging noises when the door operates.
  • Uneven door movement or sudden dropping.

Attempting DIY spring repairs is dangerous and not recommended. Licensed Miramar technicians have the tools and training to perform these replacements safely and effectively.

Emergency Garage Door Situations: What to Do Until Help Arrives

Despite best preventative measures, garage door emergencies can still arise. Immediate and informed responses help minimize damage and ensure safety before professionals arrive.

Common Emergencies and Immediate Steps

  • Door Stuck Open or Closed: Disconnect the opener using the emergency release cord to manually operate the door if safe to do so.
  • Broken Spring or Cable: Keep clear of the door and do not attempt to lift it manually, as the weight balance is compromised and can cause injury.
  • Door Falling Suddenly: Clear the area immediately and call emergency service technicians to avoid accidents.
  • Malfunctioning Safety Sensors: Avoid forcing the door and check for sensor obstructions or wiring damage without touching electrical components.
