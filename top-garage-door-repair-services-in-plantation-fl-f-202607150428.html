Broken Springs and Their Impact

Garage door springs carry the heavy load of lifting and lowering the door smoothly. Over time, these springs undergo stress cycles and may snap unexpectedly, causing the door to become inoperable. Attempting to replace springs without the correct tools and expertise can lead to serious injury.

  • Torsion Springs: Mounted above the door, these store mechanical energy for counterbalancing the door’s weight.
  • Extension Springs: Located on each side of the door’s tracks, they stretch to provide lift assistance.

Professional spring replacement by Garage Door Repair of Plantation includes proper tension adjustment, use of certified components, and safety checks to prevent recurrence.

"Many accidents happen due to improper DIY spring replacement. Our technicians follow strict safety protocols, ensuring garage doors operate reliably and safely afterward," shares Michael Torres, Chief Technician.

Maintenance Tips to Extend Your Garage Door’s Lifespan

While professional inspections and repairs are key, simple routine maintenance performed by homeowners can stave off many issues and enhance safety between service visits.

Monthly Checks and Preventative Care

  • Visual Inspection: Look for signs of wear, rust, or damage to springs, cables, and rollers.
  • Test Auto-Reverse Function: Place an object under the door to ensure it reverses when closing.
  • Lubricate Moving Parts: Use manufacturer-recommended lubricants on hinges, springs, and rollers.
  • Clean Tracks and Remove Debris: Ensure tracks remain free of dirt and obstacles that could impair movement.
  • Check Weather Seals: Replace cracked or worn seals to prevent drafts and moisture intrusion.

Safety Precautions Every Garage Door Owner Should Know

Garage doors are among the heaviest moving objects in residential properties, so adhering to safety guidelines is crucial to prevent accidents. Here are key precautions recommended by Garage Door Repair of Plantation and industry experts:

  • Never attempt DIY repairs on springs or cables: These parts are under extreme tension and pose serious injury risks.
  • Keep hands and feet clear during door operation: Children should be supervised and taught to avoid playing near garage doors.
  • Regularly test safety features: Auto-reverse and photo-eye sensors must function properly to detect obstructions.
  • Secure remotes and keypads: Prevent unauthorized access by storing remotes safely and changing keypad codes periodically.
  • Schedule professional inspections: Certified technicians identify hidden hazards before they escalate.
"Safety is paramount in every job we undertake. Our technicians educate customers on maintaining a safe garage door environment and provide detailed reports on potential hazards," explains John Reynolds.
