Common Garage Door Problems and How Experts Address Them

Knowing the root causes of garage door issues empowers homeowners to act promptly. Here is a deeper dive into some common malfunctions and how professional services resolve them efficiently:

Noise and Squeaking

Noise during door operation is often caused by worn rollers, unlubricated tracks, or loose hardware. While DIY lubrication may provide a temporary fix, technicians conduct a thorough inspection to identify any damaged components that could pose safety risks if ignored.

“Ignoring noisy garage doors can result in accelerated wear and seize-ups, ultimately leading to costly replacements,” warns Garage Door Repair of Plantation’s lead technician.

Misaligned or Damaged Tracks

Tracks can become bent or misaligned due to accidents or repeated strain, causing the door to operate unevenly or not close completely. Professional repair includes carefully realigning or replacing sections to restore smooth and safe movement.

Broken or Frayed Cables

Cables are integral to the balance and movement of garage doors, bearing substantial tension. Fraying or snapping cables not only impede function but are dangerous to handle without training. Garage Door Repair of Plantation ensures safe removal and installation using high-quality replacement cables matched to door specifications.

Faulty Sensors and Safety Mechanisms

Garage doors incorporate sensors to detect obstructions and prevent accidents. When these sensors fail or become misaligned, doors may fail to close or could close unexpectedly. Technicians perform precise calibration and replacements to ensure compliance with safety standards.

Preventive Maintenance Tips from Plantation Experts

While professional repairs are essential when problems occur, regular maintenance significantly reduces breakdowns and expenses. Below are expert recommended maintenance practices to extend garage door lifespan:

  • Monthly Visual Inspection: Check for any obvious damage such as cracked panels, loose hardware, or worn springs.
  • Lubricate Moving Parts: Apply garage door-specific lubricants to rollers, hinges, springs, and tracks about twice a year.
  • Test Balance: Disconnect the opener and manually raise the door halfway. It should hold position without falling or rising abruptly.
  • Keep the Tracks Clean: Clear any debris that could cause jamming.
  • Check the Weather Seal: Replace if cracked or missing to prevent drafts and pest entry.
