Common Garage Door Problems Unique to Weston’s Climate and How to Prevent Them

Weston’s coastal Southeast Florida environment presents specific challenges to garage door longevity and performance. Understanding these issues helps residents and business owners take preventive measures.

1. Corrosion Due to Salt Air Exposure

The salty Atlantic air accelerates rust and corrosion on metal components such as springs, cables, hinges, and tracks, often leading to premature failure.

  • Prevention: Opt for galvanized or stainless steel parts when possible, and schedule regular lubrication with corrosion-resistant products.
  • Maintenance Tip: After storms or heavy rains, inspect door components for any signs of rust or residue build-up and wipe down exposed parts.

2. Humidity-Related Warping and Swelling

Wood garage doors or wooden trim can absorb moisture in the humid environment, causing warping, sticking, or misalignment.

  • Prevention: Use synthetic or treated wood doors designed for marine climates.
  • Maintenance Tip: Apply weather seal replacements frequently to form effective moisture barriers.

3. Impact Damage from Strong Winds and Storm Debris

Weston’s proximity to tropical storms and hurricanes means garage doors often face high wind pressures and flying debris.

  • Prevention: Install wind-rated garage doors and reinforced hardware certified for Florida building codes.
  • Maintenance Tip: Inspect after storm events and address minor dents or track damage promptly to prevent larger failures.

“Homeowners often underestimate the effect of salt and humidity until their garage door springs snap or metal parts seize,” explains David Reynolds, a seasoned garage door technician with Garage Door Repair of Weston. “Regular check-ups before storm season can save thousands in costly repairs.”
