Understanding Loan-to-Value (LTV) and After-Repair Value (ARV) in Hard Money Lending

Two critical metrics in evaluating hard money loan options are Loan-to-Value (LTV) and After-Repair Value (ARV). These factors determine the maximum loan amount you can obtain and the lender’s risk exposure.

Loan-to-Value (LTV)

LTV is the ratio of the loan amount to the property’s current appraised value or purchase price. For hard money lenders, this is typically based on the property’s ARV rather than just its purchase price.

Example: If a property’s ARV is $500,000 and the lender offers 70% LTV, the maximum loan amount would be $350,000.

After-Repair Value (ARV)

ARV is the estimated market value of the property after all renovations and improvements are complete. Accurate ARV assessments are vital because lenders base loan approvals and amounts on this figure.

Investors should obtain professional appraisals or market comps to validate ARV estimates. Overestimating ARV can result in loan denials, underfunding, or financial losses if the property does not sell for the anticipated price.

Private lenders in Nevada are generally required to comply with state licensing requirements, including registration with the Nevada Division of Mortgage Lending. Ensuring your lender is licensed helps protect against predatory practices.

Nevada enforces usury laws that cap the maximum allowable interest rates for different types of loans. While hard money loans often fall into exceptions due to their secured nature, investors should verify that proposed rates are lawful and fair.

Disclosure Requirements

Lenders must provide clear disclosures regarding loan fees, interest, prepayment penalties, and other terms. Investors should review loan agreements carefully and consult legal counsel if necessary.
