Understanding Impact-Resistant Glass Technology

Impact-resistant glass is engineered with multiple layers that absorb and distribute the energy from impacts. This glass typically uses a laminated combination of two or more panes fused with a vinyl interlayer. When struck, the interlayer holds shards together, preventing dangerous breakage and maintaining a barrier against wind and water.

Florida window contractors leverage this technology to not only comply with Florida Building Code requirements but also enhance energy efficiency. According to the U.S. Department of Energy, properly installed impact-resistant windows can reduce energy loss by up to 30%, decreasing both utility bills and the environmental footprint of homes.

Maintenance Tips to Extend the Life of Impact Windows

Proper maintenance is key to ensuring your impact windows continue to perform optimally over many years, especially given Florida’s harsh weather conditions. Regular care can prevent minor issues from escalating into costly repairs or replacements.

Routine Inspection and Cleaning

  • Visual Inspection: Conduct quarterly inspections of your windows, looking for cracks in the glass, gaps in the seals, corrosion on frames, or damaged hardware.
  • Gentle Cleaning: Use mild, non-abrasive cleaners and soft cloths to clean both the glass and frames. Avoid harsh chemicals that can degrade window coatings or seals.
  • Track and Sill Care: Clear debris from window tracks and sills to ensure smooth operation and prevent water buildup that can cause rot or rust.

Maintaining clean and debris-free components enhances window durability and maintains aesthetic appeal.

Seal and Weatherstripping Maintenance

Flashing and weatherstripping form the first line of defense against moisture intrusion and air leakage. These elements endure wear over time, and replacement is essential to maintain impact window effectiveness.

  • Check seals for brittleness, cracking, or peeling, especially after storm seasons.
  • Replace weatherstripping annually or as needed with high-quality, UV-resistant materials.
  • Ensure caulking around window frames remains intact and reseal any gaps promptly.

Proper sealing prevents water penetration, reduces energy loss, and minimizes mold development risks.

Hardware and Lock Functionality

Locks and opening mechanisms should be examined and lubricated regularly to maintain security and ease of use.

  • Inspect locking points and hinges for rust or stiffness.
  • Apply silicone-based lubricants to moving parts to prevent corrosion.
  • Address any malfunction promptly to avoid compromising window operability and security.
