Common Garage Door Problems and How Professionals Address Them

Understanding typical garage door issues and the professional repairs required will help you recognize when to call an expert, preventing further damage or hazards.

Broken Springs

Garage door springs bear significant tension to counterbalance the door's weight. When springs break, the door becomes heavy or immovable, posing serious safety risks. Professionals carefully replace springs with exact tension specifications to restore proper balance and function.

Misaligned or Damaged Tracks

If your door does not open or close smoothly, tracks may be bent or loose. Technicians straighten, realign, and securely fasten tracks to ensure smooth, stable movement without strain on the opener.

Faulty Garage Door Openers

Common opener problems include motor failures, worn gears, or circuit board damage. Certified repair specialists diagnose electrical or mechanical faults, replacing components promptly to restore reliable operation.

Worn Out Rollers and Hinges

Squeaky or jerky door motion often results from failing rollers or hinges. Experienced technicians replace these parts with high-quality components, often upgrading to nylon rollers for quieter, longer-lasting performance.

Sensor and Safety Device Malfunctions

Garage doors feature sensors to prevent accidents by reversing if obstructions are detected. Professionals test, recalibrate, or replace sensors ensuring compliance with safety standards and your family’s protection.

Tips for DIY Garage Door Troubleshooting Before Calling a Professional

While many garage door issues require expert attention, some minor problems can be diagnosed or temporarily mitigated by homeowners to avoid emergency calls.

  • Check Power Supply: Confirm the opener is plugged in and circuit breakers are not tripped.
  • Inspect Remote Batteries: Replace batteries in key fobs or wall controls if unresponsive.
  • Visual Track Inspection: Look for obvious obstructions, debris, or panel damage around tracks.
  • Listen for Unusual Sounds: Grinding or squeaking may indicate lubrication needs or component wear.
  • Test Photo Sensors: Ensure safety sensors are aligned and free from dirt blocking the infrared beam.
