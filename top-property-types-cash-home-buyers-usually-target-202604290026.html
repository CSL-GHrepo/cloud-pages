Quick Answer: What Types of Properties Do Cash Home Buyers Usually Purchase?

Cash home buyers often focus on properties that offer the potential for quick turnaround and flexibility, including distressed properties, foreclosure homes, inherited properties, fixer-uppers, vacant houses, and probate sales. These types tend to involve motivated sellers who want to avoid lengthy listing processes or costly repairs, making them perfect candidates for cash transactions.

"Cash buyers are especially attracted to properties where traditional financing hurdles can slow down or jeopardize a sale—distressed and foreclosure homes top that list," says real estate analyst Maxine Rivera.

Why Do Cash Home Buyers Target Specific Property Types?

Cash home buyers seek properties that align with the goals of speedy, low-complication sales. Below are the reasons these property types are most attractive:

  • Time Sensitivity: Sellers of distressed or inherited properties often need fast cash to resolve financial or personal situations.
  • Avoiding Repairs and Renovations: Many cash buyers specialize in homes that require work, alleviating sellers from investing in improvements.
  • Financing Challenges: Foreclosure homes or probate sales might not qualify for traditional mortgages quickly, so a cash buyer can bypass this hurdle.
  • Reduced Transaction Complexity: Cash sales often close faster and with less paperwork, ideal for sellers who prioritize speed.

Top Property Types Cash Home Buyers Usually Target

1. Distressed Properties

These are homes in poor condition due to neglect, damage, or financial hardship. Cash home buyers pursue distressed properties because they can acquire them below market value and either renovate or resell quickly.

According to a report by ATTOM Data Solutions, about 24% of the US homes purchased by investors in 2023 were distressed or in need of repairs.

Examples include homes with roof leaks, outdated electrical systems, or water damage. Sellers often opt for cash buyers to avoid costly repair mandates required by traditional lenders.

2. Foreclosure Homes

Foreclosure homes are properties repossessed by lenders due to the homeowner’s failure to pay the mortgage. These homes can be sold quickly by banks or trustees to recover losses. Cash buyers are well-positioned to move fast on foreclosures without waiting for mortgage approvals.

Foreclosure sales account for a significant market share of quick sale real estate since sellers (often banks or distressed owners) prioritize speed over price.

3. Inherited Properties & Probate Sales

Inherited properties present unique challenges. They often become part of probate sales, where the estate must be settled and sold. Heirs may be motivated to liquidate the property quickly, especially if it is distant or requires upkeep.

Cash home buyers offer a streamlined option, able to purchase "as-is" without lengthy probate court processes.

4. Fixer-Uppers

Homes that need substantial renovations or cosmetic updates naturally attract cash buyers who specialize in rehab projects. These buyers can handle major repairs without dependency on mortgage contingencies, which traditional buyers might struggle with.

For owners overwhelmed by the work or cost, cash buyers provide relief and a fast sale.

5. Vacant Houses

Vacant homes pose risks such as vandalism, squatting, or rapid deterioration. Owners frequently want a quick sale to avoid these liabilities.

Cash buyers can act decisively, making quick offers to avoid prolonged vacancy periods.

6. Rental Property Investors Looking to Sell

While cash home buyers primarily target distressed or complicated properties, they also purchase rental properties. Investors who want a quick exit from the rental market might prefer cash offers to bypass tenant-related complications and lengthy listing processes.
