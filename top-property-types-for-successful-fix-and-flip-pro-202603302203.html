Quick Answer: What Are the Best Types of Properties for Fix and Flip?

The top property types for fix and flip projects in 2024 are single-family homes and fixer-upper homes in stable or improving neighborhoods. These properties typically offer predictable renovation scopes and strong buyer demand, ensuring a favorable property resale value. Multi-family units and select townhomes can also be lucrative depending on local market trends and investor expertise.

"Single-family homes remain the backbone of successful renovation projects due to their universal appeal and market liquidity," says Sarah Kim, a seasoned real estate investment consultant.

Why Single-Family Homes Dominate Fix and Flip Success

Single-family homes are arguably the most popular property type for fix and flip investors—and with good reason. They generally have broad market appeal, attracting first-time homebuyers, young families, and retirees alike. This translates into quicker sales and often higher resale values after renovations than many other property types.

Key advantages of single-family homes for fix and flip projects:

  • Market Size: Comprise the largest segment of the residential market, enhancing buyer demand.
  • Predictable Renovation Scope: Renovation projects are usually straightforward—kitchens, bathrooms, flooring—which helps control costs.
  • Appreciation Potential: Historically strong property resale value in growing neighborhoods.

According to the National Association of Realtors (NAR), single-family home sales accounted for 75% of all existing-home sales in 2023, emphasizing their dominant role in real estate investment and flip houses.

Fixer-Upper Homes: High Risk, High Reward

Fixer-upper homes involve properties that need significant repairs or updates but are often priced below market value. These can provide substantial profit margins if renovation projects are managed efficiently.

When selecting fixer-uppers for a fix_and_flip project, investors should consider:

  • Structural Integrity: Avoid homes with foundational or major structural problems unless experienced with costly repairs.
  • Location: Neighborhoods with steady or rising home values ensure the effort adds appreciable resale value.
  • Scope & Budgeting: Detailed cost estimates prevent budget overruns.

Industry data from ATTOM Data Solutions shows that the average ROI on fix-and-flip homes in 2023 reached nearly 45%, but fixer-uppers with manageable renovation projects consistently outperformed those requiring extensive structural work.

Other Investment Property Types Worth Considering

While single-family and fixer-upper homes are proven staples, other types can be lucrative in specific contexts:

  • Multi-Family Units: Duplexes and triplexes can generate rental income if a full resale is delayed and provide multiple living spaces at once. These are best suited to experienced investors comfortable with more complex renovations.
  • Townhomes and Condominiums: Often located in urban or suburban developments where space is limited. Renovation projects here typically focus on interiors and amenities, appealing to younger buyers and downsizers.
  • New Construction Resale: Buying new builds in emerging areas, renovating for upgrades or finishes, and flipping quickly before the market saturates.

Market Trends 2024: What to Watch for When Choosing Your Property

Market conditions significantly impact fix and flip profitability. Key trends influencing the best types of investment property types include:

  • Rising Interest Rates: Increasing rates have slightly cooled buyer demand, emphasizing the importance of selecting properties that attract first-time buyers at affordable prices.
  • Suburban Growth: Many buyers are moving from urban centers seeking more space, pushing demand for single-family homes in suburban neighborhoods.
  • Supply Chain Improvements: Renovation materials are becoming more accessible, helping keep renovation timelines shorter and costs more predictable.

Investors focusing on adaptable property types like single-family homes with moderate renovation requirements are positioned best to capitalize on these trends.

What makes single-family homes the best choice for fix and flip projects?

Are fixer-upper homes always a good option for flip houses?

Fixer-upper homes can deliver high returns but come with higher renovation risks, especially if they have structural issues. Can multi-family properties be suitable for fix and flip projects?

Yes, multi-family properties such as duplexes or triplexes can be profitable, especially for investors experienced with renovation complexity. They can generate rental income if resale is delayed. However, they often require more capital and longer project timelines compared to single-family homes, so careful market analysis and expertise are essential.

Rising interest rates have reduced affordability, emphasizing the need to select properties appealing to first-time buyers, primarily single-family homes in suburban areas. Supply chain improvements aid renovation timelines, and suburban growth drives demand. Aligning property type choice with these trends helps maximize the potential resale value and overall success of fix and flip projects.
