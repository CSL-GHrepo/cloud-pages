Understanding the Main Types of Copier Leasing Agreements

1. Operating Lease

The operating lease is the most common type of copier equipment leasing contract offered by vendors. It resembles a rental agreement, where the lessee rents the copier for a fixed period, typically 2-5 years. The lessor retains ownership throughout the lease term.

  • Key Features: Lower monthly payments, off-balance-sheet leasing, short to medium term.
  • Advantages: Flexibility to upgrade equipment regularly, maintenance often included, minimal upfront cost.
  • Typical Use Case: Businesses that want to avoid depreciation concerns and keep copier technology current.

2. Capital Lease (Finance Lease)

A capital lease functions more like a loan. It conveys ownership or gives the lessee the option to own the copier at the end of the lease. Monthly payments are higher, reflecting amortization of the copier’s full cost.

  • Key Features: Lease term usually matches the copier’s useful life, lessee records asset on balance sheet.
  • Advantages: Potential tax benefits like depreciation, eventual ownership, fixed assets for business.
  • Typical Use Case: Businesses that prefer owning their equipment and can handle larger upfront liabilities.

3. Fair Market Value (FMV) Lease

An FMV lease is a subtype of operating lease with an option to purchase the copier at its fair market value at the lease end. This provides maximum flexibility and allows companies to decide based on the copier’s residual value.

4. Lease with Buyout Clause

Some commercial copier leasing plans include a lease buyout clause, where the business can purchase the copier at a predetermined price before or at lease expiry. This helps manage long-term budgeting and ownership goals.

Operating Lease vs Capital Lease: Which Is Better for Your Business?

Industry professionals recommend weighing your business’s financial structure and objectives carefully. An operating lease preserves working capital and keeps liabilities off your balance sheet, improving financial ratios. A capital lease adds assets but increases liabilities, which can impact credit availability.

"For most small to midsize businesses, operating leases deliver the best balance of cost, flexibility, and technology access," says Richard Cowell, Leasing Consultant at Office Solutions.

Consider these factors when choosing between operating and capital leases:

  • Balance Sheet Impact: Operating leases do not appear as liabilities under most accounting standards.
  • Tax Treatment: Payments on operating leases may be fully deductible as a business expense.
  • Upgrade Frequency: Operating leases facilitate frequent equipment refreshes.
  • Ownership Intent: Capital leases typically end with transferable ownership.

Critical Components in Copier Lease Terms and Conditions

The devil is in the details when it comes to copier leasing agreements. Understanding your copier lease terms and conditions is essential to avoid unexpected costs or operational setbacks. Here are key contract clauses to scrutinize:

  • Lease Duration: Typical terms range from 24 to 60 months; shorter terms may cost more monthly.
  • Monthly Payment Structure: Fixed payments versus variable rates tied to usage or maintenance.
  • Maintenance and Service: Who covers repairs and parts? Full-service agreements vs. pay-as-you-go service.
  • Usage Limits: Page/month allowances, charges for excess usage.
  • End-of-Lease Options: Renewal, purchase, or return policies.
  • Lease Buyout Clause: Flexibility to purchase the copier at a specified price.
  • Early Termination Fees: Potential penalties if the lease ends before the agreed term.
