Common Property Types Purchased by Cash Home Buyers

1. Distressed Properties

Distressed properties are homes suffering from financial, legal, or physical issues. This can mean outstanding liens, overdue taxes, or homes in severe disrepair. Cash buyers take interest in these because they can acquire them below market value and often restore them rapidly.

  • Examples include homes with plumbing or electrical damage, extensive mold, or structural issues.
  • According to ATTOM Data Solutions, 5.6% of all US residential properties were distressed sales in 2023, showing a significant market for cash buyers.

2. Foreclosure Homes

Foreclosures occur when the homeowner fails to make mortgage payments, leading to the bank repossessing the property. These homes often sell at auctions or through banks seeking a quick sale to limit losses. Cash buyers focus on foreclosures because banks prefer quick, hassle-free transactions without waiting for loan approvals.

3. Probate and Inherited Properties

When a property owner dies, their estate often enters probate, a legal process settling debts and distributing assets. Inherited properties can be burdensome for heirs who lack the desire or resources to manage them, making cash_home_buyer services highly attractive.

  • Inherited property buyers pay cash for offer convenience and quick closing.
  • Regionally, probate proceedings vary, but the quick turnaround of cash buyers benefits sellers nationwide.

4. Fixer-Upper Houses

Fixer-uppers are homes needing cosmetic or structural renovation. Investors seek these homes to renovate and sell for a profit or rent out. Sellers often choose cash buyers to offload properties “as-is” without making repairs themselves, saving time and money.

5. Vacant Land Sales

Empty lots or undeveloped land may attract cash buyers looking for land to develop, resell, or hold as investment. These transactions avoid the complexity of habitable home sales but still require fast closures favored by cash buyers.

6. Off-Market Homes

Off-market properties aren’t actively listed publicly but may be available through direct owner negotiations. Cash buyers often specialize in locating these hidden opportunities, benefiting sellers looking for discretion or speed.

Are cash home buyers interested in fixer-upper houses or only move-in ready homes?

How do foreclosure homes differ from other distressed properties for cash buyers?
