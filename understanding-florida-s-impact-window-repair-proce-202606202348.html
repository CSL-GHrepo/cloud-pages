Florida Impact Window Repair: Step-by-Step Process

Repairing impact windows in Florida involves several distinct steps, each designed to ensure safety and compliance:

  1. Initial Damage Inspection: A certified impact window technician assesses the damage’s extent, focusing on cracks, frame deformation, and seal integrity.
  2. Assessment Against Florida Codes: The damage and repair method must comply with 2024 Florida building codes and hurricane impact window guidelines, specifically addressing impact resistance and energy efficiency.
  3. Obtain Necessary Permits: Depending on the scope, local municipalities may require a permit verifying the work adheres to code.
  4. Performing Repairs Using Certified Materials: Replacement glass and frame materials must be certified to Florida standards and maintain previous impact ratings.
  5. Post-Repair Inspection and Certification: A final inspection ensures repairs restore or exceed the original impact window certification Florida expects, qualifying the window for wind mitigation credits on insurance.

Choosing an experienced Florida impact window repair professional is critical. They understand nuances such as proper sealants resistant to Florida humidity and UV damage, as well as how to maintain a window’s structural integrity against future storms.

Impact Window Installation Standards Florida Relate to Repairs

Impact window installation standards in Florida are governed by the Florida Building Code (FBC), which was significantly updated in 2024 to enhance storm resilience:

  • Glazing Material Requirements: Only impact-resistant laminated glass meeting ASTM E1886 and E1996 standards is approved.
  • Frame and Anchorage: Frames must resist hurricane-force winds, typically tested for ASTM structural performance.
  • Energy Efficiency: Windows must also comply with energy efficiency standards, including Low-E coatings, relevant for Florida’s climate.

Repairs must restore the window to these exacting standards. For example, replacing broken glass with a non-certified product disqualifies the window from certification and may invalidate insurance claims post-storm.

“According to the Florida Department of Business & Professional Regulation, restoring impact windows to current installation standards is essential for maintaining a home’s wind mitigation rating and insurance eligibility.”
