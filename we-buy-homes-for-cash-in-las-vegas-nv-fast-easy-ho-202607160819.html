How Cash Home Sales Impact Your Taxes in Nevada

Selling your home for cash in Nevada can have tax implications that are important to understand:

  • Capital Gains Tax: If the home is your primary residence, you may exclude up to $250,000 ($500,000 for married couples) in capital gains from federal taxes, regardless of how you sell.
  • Reporting the Sale: Even when selling to a cash buyer, the transaction must be reported to the IRS, typically using IRS Form 1099-S, handled by the title company or escrow agent.
  • State Taxes: Nevada does not have a state income tax, meaning no state capital gains tax on home sales.
  • Potential Property Tax Prorations: Closing quickly may affect how property taxes are prorated between buyer and seller, often handled during closing.

Consulting a qualified tax advisor or accountant familiar with Nevada real estate sales is advised to understand your individual tax situation fully. “Cash sale proceeds do not inherently trigger different tax liabilities than financed sales, but knowing your exemptions and reporting requirements is essential,” says CPA Ramon Delgado of Las Vegas.
