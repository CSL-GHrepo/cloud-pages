Tax Implications When Selling a Home for Cash in Parkland

Selling a home for cash in Parkland, FL, like any real estate transaction, carries potential tax implications that sellers should understand. While the method of payment (cash versus financed offer) does not directly affect taxes, the sale itself triggers specific tax considerations.

Capital Gains Tax

If the home has appreciated in value since purchase, sellers may owe capital gains tax on the profit. However, the IRS allows certain exclusions under IRS Section 121, such as:

  • Primary Residence Exclusion: Individuals can exclude up to $250,000 of capital gains if they've lived in the home for at least two of the past five years; married couples filing jointly may exclude up to $500,000.
  • Investment Properties: Properties not used as primary residences typically don’t qualify for exclusions and may be subject to full capital gains tax.

1031 Exchange Option

For sellers with investment properties in Parkland, a 1031 exchange may be a useful strategy to defer capital gains tax by reinvesting proceeds into a similar property. This must be carefully planned and executed within IRS rules.

State and Local Tax Considerations

Florida does not impose a state income tax, which benefits sellers by reducing the overall tax burden. However, sellers should still account for:

  • Property Taxes: Ensure property taxes are up to date before closing, as unpaid taxes could become a lien.
  • Documentary Stamp Taxes: In Florida, the documentary stamp tax applies on deeds and certain documents during real estate transfers. Typically, this cost falls on the seller or is negotiated in the sale agreement.

Consulting a tax professional familiar with Parkland’s local regulations ensures accurate tax planning and compliance during the sale.
